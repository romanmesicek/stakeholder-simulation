# Event Cards

Events the facilitator can inject during negotiations to shift leverage, break deadlocks, or add realism. Read the card text aloud as "breaking news". Cards are asymmetric by design — they shift leverage, they do not reset the game.

## Overview

| No. | Event | Effect |
|-----|-------|--------|
| E1 | **Near-blackout:** A heat wave plus an unplanned outage nearly causes a regional blackout | Strengthens reliability and timeline arguments |
| E2 | **Negative watch:** A rating agency puts PowerShift on "negative watch" | Cost pressure on Management, leverage for Investors |
| E3 | **Faster coal exit:** The national government drafts a law pulling the coal exit forward by two years | Time pressure on everyone, tailwind for Environmental |
| E4 | **Mercury report:** Investigative journalists report elevated mercury levels near the Northern Plant | Restoration and Indigenous claims gain urgency |
| E5 | **EU funding window:** €150 million EU Just Transition co-funding — but only for a jointly agreed plan | Common gain that rewards agreement, deadline pressure |
| E6 | **Rival region:** A neighboring region offers subsidies and fast-track permits for the renewables investment | Exit leverage for Management and Investors |

## Usage Recommendations

**When to use?**
- When negotiations stall or coalitions have frozen prematurely
- When one side is dominating and the balance needs shifting
- Best window: Rounds 3–4 of the negotiation phase; avoid Round 5 (final agreements)

**Sequencing:**
- E1/E3 work early — they reframe the timeline debate before coalitions harden
- E2/E6 help when Management or Investors are being steamrolled
- E4 helps when Environmental, Indigenous, or Community are being ignored
- E5 is the closer — use it in Round 4 when a deal is close but nobody wants to move first

**Important:** Use at most 2 cards per session. Pick what fits the current state of play — no card is mandatory. After injecting, give groups 2 minutes to caucus before negotiations resume.

## Detailed Descriptions

### E1: Near-blackout — Reliability becomes real

> Breaking news: During this week's heat wave, an unplanned outage at a neighboring gas plant pushed the regional grid below its N-1 contingency margin for 40 minutes. Operators shed industrial load to prevent a cascading blackout. The regulator demands proof that the coal phase-out plan maintains the 18% reserve margin at every stage.

**Effect:** Reliability stops being an abstract argument. The Technical Expert Panel's feasibility criteria gain enforcement power; positions demanding very fast closure lose ground; a phased, storage-backed plan gains support.

### E2: Negative watch — The financial clock ticks

> Breaking news: A major rating agency has placed PowerShift Energy on "negative watch", citing transition uncertainty and escalating stakeholder costs. A downgrade below investment grade would raise financing costs for the entire €3 billion program by an estimated €40–60 million per year.

**Effect:** Management and Investors gain a concrete argument for cost discipline and a swift, credible deal — prolonged conflict now has a price tag. Other groups can counter: only a broadly supported agreement calms the markets; a cheap deal that collapses politically is the worst outcome for the rating.

### E3: Faster coal exit — Politics accelerates

> Breaking news: The national government has published a draft law pulling the coal exit forward by two years, citing EU pressure and missed interim targets. It is not yet passed — but regions that submit credible, broadly supported transition plans will be granted flexibility in implementation.

**Effect:** Time pressure on everyone. Environmental gains momentum; Management, Workers and Community must weigh whether a negotiated deal now beats a dictated deadline later. Government can present itself as the channel for regional flexibility — if the region speaks with one voice.

### E4: Mercury report — The environmental bill arrives

> Breaking news: An investigative report documents elevated mercury levels in soil and groundwater near the Northern Plant — including in the river system running through Indigenous territory. Experts expect remediation costs well above the current decommissioning budget line. National media have picked up the story.

**Effect:** Restoration funding moves to the top of the agenda; the €400 million decommissioning-and-restoration line is now visibly contested. Indigenous and Environmental claims gain documented urgency; Management comes under reputational pressure to make binding, monitored commitments.

### E5: EU funding window — A reward for agreeing

> Breaking news: The EU Just Transition Fund has opened a call: the region can receive up to €150 million in co-funding for worker retraining, community development, and site restoration — but only for a plan jointly endorsed by the key stakeholders and certified as feasible, submitted by the end of this conference.

**Effect:** Fresh money that only exists if the parties agree — and it routes through the Feasibility Certificate, strengthening the Technical Panel. Shrinks the distribution conflict and rewards coalition-building. The ideal card when a deal is close but nobody wants to move first.

### E6: Rival region — The investment could leave

> Breaking news: The economic development agency of a neighboring region has publicly offered PowerShift a subsidy package and fast-track permitting if it sites its new wind and solar capacity there instead. Their press release calls it "a home for investment that is welcome".

**Effect:** Management and Investors gain exit leverage: local siting of the renewables — and the construction jobs and tax base that come with it — is no longer guaranteed. Community and Government must decide what local siting is worth; Workers face the prospect of replacement jobs emerging elsewhere.
