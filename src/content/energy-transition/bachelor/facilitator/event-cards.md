# Event Cards

Events the facilitator can inject during negotiations to shift leverage, break deadlocks, or add realism. Read the card text aloud as "breaking news".

## Overview

| No. | Event | Effect |
|-----|-------|--------|
| E1 | **Near-blackout:** A heat wave plus an unplanned outage nearly causes a regional blackout | Strengthens reliability and timeline arguments |
| E2 | **Negative watch:** A rating agency puts PowerShift on "negative watch" | Cost pressure on Management, leverage for Investors |
| E3 | **Faster coal exit:** The national government drafts a law pulling the coal exit forward by two years | Time pressure on everyone, tailwind for Environmental |
| E4 | **Mercury report:** Investigative journalists report elevated mercury levels near the Northern Plant | Restoration demands gain urgency, reputation damage |
| E5 | **EU funding window:** €150 million EU Just Transition co-funding — but only for a jointly agreed plan | Common gain that rewards agreement, deadline pressure |
| E6 | **Rival region:** A neighboring region offers subsidies and fast-track permits for the renewables investment | Exit leverage for Management and Investors |

## Usage Recommendations

**When to use?**
- When negotiations stall or a group has run out of arguments
- When one side is dominating and the balance needs shifting
- Around the middle of Phase 3 (minute 10–20), never in the final five minutes

**Sequencing:**
- E1/E3 work early — they reframe the timeline debate
- E2/E6 help when Management or Investors are being steamrolled
- E4 helps when Environmental or Community are being ignored
- E5 is the closer — use it when a deal is close but nobody wants to move first

**Important:** In the 35-minute bachelor negotiation phase, use at most 1–2 cards. Pick what fits the current state of play — no card is mandatory.

## Detailed Descriptions

### E1: Near-blackout — Reliability becomes real

> Breaking news: During this week's heat wave, an unplanned outage at a neighboring gas plant pushed the regional grid to the edge of a blackout. Operators shed industrial load for 40 minutes. Regulators demand proof that the coal phase-out plan protects supply security.

**Effect:** Reliability stops being an abstract argument. Positions demanding very fast closure lose ground; a phased, storage-backed plan gains support.

### E2: Negative watch — The financial clock ticks

> Breaking news: A major rating agency has placed PowerShift Energy on "negative watch", citing transition uncertainty and rising stakeholder costs. A downgrade would raise borrowing costs for the entire €3 billion program.

**Effect:** Management and Investors gain a concrete argument for cost discipline and a credible deal — prolonged conflict now costs money. Other groups can counter: only a broadly supported agreement calms the markets.

### E3: Faster coal exit — Politics accelerates

> Breaking news: The national government has published a draft law pulling the coal exit forward by two years. It is not yet passed — but the direction is clear, and regions with credible transition plans will be treated more favorably.

**Effect:** Time pressure on everyone. Environmental gains momentum; Management and Workers must weigh whether a negotiated deal now beats a dictated deadline later.

### E4: Mercury report — The environmental bill arrives

> Breaking news: An investigative report documents elevated mercury levels in soil and groundwater near the Northern Plant. Experts expect remediation costs well above previous estimates. National media have picked up the story.

**Effect:** Restoration funding moves up the agenda; vague clean-up promises no longer suffice. Management comes under reputational pressure to make binding commitments.

### E5: EU funding window — A reward for agreeing

> Breaking news: The EU Just Transition Fund has opened a call: the region can receive up to €150 million in co-funding for worker retraining and community development — but only for a plan jointly endorsed by the key stakeholders, submitted by the end of this conference.

**Effect:** Fresh money that only exists if the parties agree. Shrinks the distribution conflict and rewards coalition-building — the ideal card when a deal is close.

### E6: Rival region — The investment could leave

> Breaking news: The economic development agency of a neighboring region has publicly offered PowerShift subsidies and fast-track permitting if it builds its new wind and solar capacity there instead.

**Effect:** Management and Investors gain exit leverage: local siting is no longer guaranteed. Community and Government must decide what local siting is worth to them.
