# Common Problems Playbook

Prefer structural fixes over in-the-moment policing. Intervene freely on **process** (time, turn-taking, calling a caucus); intervene on **content** only to clear up rule confusion.

## Setup checklist (before the session)

- [ ] Situation briefing assigned as pre-reading (reading time in-session is tight)
- [ ] Role slips prepared for random draw; groups of 3–5 per stakeholder
- [ ] Mentimeter prepared: power ratings (1–10 per stakeholder), outcome prediction (Agreement / Partial / Deadlock), plus the pre/post questions from the student guide
- [ ] Event cards printed or ready to read aloud (use at most 1–2)
- [ ] Hard stop times announced for each phase — protect the debrief block
- [ ] Decide who chairs Phases 2 and 4: you, in the side-role of Regional Energy Minister

## The problems

### 1. One group dominates everything

**Signs:** One table talks in every exchange; others wait passively; the same two voices carry each round.
**Do:** Enforce turn order in plenary moments. Call a caucus break and visit quiet tables with one prompt ("What would you need to say yes?"). If one *person* dominates within a group, assign sub-roles (lead negotiator, analyst, note-taker) and rotate the lead.

### 2. A group refuses to play its role

**Signs:** "We wouldn't actually want this"; arguing personal opinions; giving away confidential constraints for laughs.
**Do:** Reframe privately: "You're the board's delegation — argue what they would argue." Point them to one concrete card element (a goal, a constraint) as their next move. Don't debate the role's ethics mid-game; park it explicitly for the debrief.

### 3. Consensus arrives suspiciously early

**Signs:** A "deal" in the first ten minutes; no numbers attached; everyone relieved.
**Do:** Stress-test it in role: "Where does the money come from — which source funds each promise?" Point groups back to their constraints and Avoid lines. If it still holds with named funding, fine — deepen it (timeline milestones, enforcement). Usually it collapses on the first number.

### 4. Total deadlock

**Signs:** Repetition of positions; nobody offers; groups disengage.
**Do:** Call a 3-minute caucus with one instruction: "Come back with one conditional offer: 'We could accept X if Y.'" Or inject an event card that shifts leverage (E5 — the EU funding window — is built for this). Remind the room what the no-deal default costs *them*, group by group.

### 5. Escalation gets personal

**Signs:** Interrupting, mocking, attributing bad faith to classmates rather than roles.
**Do:** Interrupt on process, not content: "Attack positions, not people — that's a ground rule." Physically separate the parties for one bilateral round. Name it briefly and move on; treat the underlying conflict as material for the debrief, not a discipline problem.

### 6. The clock beats the debrief

**Signs:** Negotiations overrun; debrief shrinks to five minutes.
**Do:** Don't. The debrief is where the learning consolidates — its time block is protected. Cut negotiation Round 2 short instead; an unfinished negotiation debriefs just as well as a finished one.
