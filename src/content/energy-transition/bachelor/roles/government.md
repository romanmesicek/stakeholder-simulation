# Regional Government
## Stakeholder Role Card

---

### WHO YOU ARE

You represent the regional government, responsible for balancing economic development, social welfare, environmental protection, and energy security. You must keep everyone reasonably satisfied while meeting legal obligations.

**Your authority:** You decide on permits, control the €500 million transition fund, run retraining through the technical colleges, and convened this conference. Every Deal Sheet that counts needs your signature.

**Who you answer to:** The regional parliament (your coalition holds a slim majority), the national government and its climate law, and the voters, who elect you again in 24 months.

---

### YOUR SITUATION

**Political Reality:**
- Election in 24 months; how you handle the transition decides the outcome
- Northern Town + Southern Region = 23,000 residents in swing constituencies
- Coalition government with internal tensions
- Media scrutiny is intense

**Legal Obligations:**
- Climate law: 45% emissions reduction by 2030
- Energy security: 99.97% availability required
- Social protection: Prevent mass unemployment
- Indigenous rights: the constitution requires consultation on development on Indigenous land

**Budget:**
- €500 million transition fund available
- Emergency reserve: €150 million (only if a crisis is visible)
- Maximum total: €650 million across all packages

---

### YOUR GOALS

1. **Energy security:** No blackouts for 350,000 homes
2. **Prevent social crisis:** Avoid mass unemployment
3. **Meet climate targets:** 45% reduction by 2030

---

### YOUR RESOURCES

**What you control:**
- €500-650 million public funding
- Permit approval/denial authority
- Retraining programs through technical colleges
- Economic development support
- Political leverage and media access

**What you can offer:**
- Public funding for worker, community and restoration packages
- Fast-track permits (saves the company €30-50 million)
- Employment services
- Infrastructure investment
- An "honest broker" role in negotiations

---

### YOUR CONSTRAINTS (Non-Negotiable)

- **Energy security:** Cannot risk blackouts
- **Climate compliance:** €50 million annual penalties if targets are missed
- **Budget limit:** Cannot exceed €650 million in total
- **Social stability:** Cannot accept a visible social crisis

---

### KEY FACTS TO USE

- Rushed 4-year transitions elsewhere caused 47-hour outages
- €500 million = 18% of the annual regional budget (a significant commitment)
- €50 million annual penalties for missing climate targets
- 23,000 residents in affected communities

---

### SUCCESS FOR YOU

**Best:** 6-year transition. Energy security maintained. Climate targets met. Public share at or under €500 million.

**Acceptable:** 5-7 years. Up to €650 million public money. Manageable opposition.

**Avoid:** Blackouts. Social crisis. Over €650 million cost. Electoral catastrophe.

**Your no-deal outcome:** The region misses its binding 45% target: €50 million annual penalties, public blame, and the social fallout still lands on you.

---

### NEGOTIATION TIPS

- **Your money is the biggest pot for packages,** and everyone will ask for it. Decide early how you split the €500 million, and tie each euro to something in return (no strikes, sites, timeline).
- **You and Management must both sign.** If you two cannot agree, there is no deal at all.
- **Trade-offs to consider:** paying more yourself vs. pushing costs to the company; a fast timeline for the climate target vs. a slower one that avoids social crisis.
- **Your vulnerability:** Every group expects you to be fair. Taking sides too early costs you the broker role.

---

### USEFUL PHRASES

- "Government is committed to a just transition protecting workers and communities..."
- "We offer €500 million: 18% of our budget shows serious commitment..."
- "Energy security is non-negotiable: blackouts create legal liability..."
- "Climate targets are law, not choice. We face €50 million penalties..."
