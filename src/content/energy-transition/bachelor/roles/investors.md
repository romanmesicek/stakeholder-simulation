# Investor Coalition
## Stakeholder Role Card

---

### WHO YOU ARE

You represent institutional investors (pension funds, asset managers) who together own 68% of PowerShift Energy. You manage money for millions of pensioners and savers who depend on returns for their retirement.

**Your authority:** Your 68% majority elects the Board and approves major investments. The transition needs €750 million in new equity (fresh shareholder money) as part of its financing, and only your vote releases it. Any company spending above €3.1 billion needs your approval.

**Who you answer to:** About 4 million beneficiaries, mostly pensioners, and the trustees of your funds, who are legally bound to protect their money (fiduciary duty).

---

### YOUR SITUATION

**Your Investment:**
- Company value: €4.2 billion
- Share price down 18% since the transition was announced
- The coal plants are a €400-800 million stranded-asset risk (value that may simply disappear)

**Your analysts' return rule:** At the planned €3.0 billion company spend, the transition returns about 9.5% a year. Every extra €100 million the company spends lowers that by about 0.8 percentage points. Money from the government fund or EU programs does not count against the return. In practice:

| Company spend | Projected return |
|---------------|------------------|
| €2.8 billion | about 11% |
| €3.0 billion | about 9.5% |
| €3.1 billion | about 8.7% |
| €3.3 billion | about 7% |
| €3.5 billion | about 5.5% |

**Investor Priorities:**
- Pension funds: need stable returns for retirees
- ESG-focused investors: will sell their shares if climate performance stays poor
- All: need a transition that creates value, not destroys it

---

### YOUR GOALS

1. **Protect shareholder value:** Returns must stay above the 8% hurdle rate
2. **Strong ESG performance:** ESG rating upgraded within 24 months
3. **Governance accountability:** Milestones, reporting, executive pay linked to delivery

---

### YOUR RESOURCES

**What you control:**
- 68% voting majority (Board, major investments)
- The €750 million in new equity the transition needs
- Approval of any company spending above €3.1 billion
- Divestment: if you sell, the share price crashes and borrowing gets more expensive for the company

**What you can offer:**
- Capital approval for the transition
- Patience on the timeline (within reason)
- Governance support for management
- Positive market signals

---

### YOUR CONSTRAINTS (Non-Negotiable)

- **Fiduciary duty:** You must put your beneficiaries' financial interests first
- **Minimum 8% return:** Below this, the investment destroys value
- **Keep the credit rating:** A downgrade would put €400 million of company bonds at risk of early repayment and raise all borrowing costs
- **Governance mechanisms:** Milestones, accountability, reporting

---

### KEY FACTS TO USE

- We represent 4 million beneficiaries, pensioners depending on returns
- Climate risk is financial risk: ESG is risk management
- Major projects average 25% cost overruns without proper governance
- Capital destroyed here is unavailable for other climate solutions

---

### SUCCESS FOR YOU

**Best:** 7-year timeline. Return 10%+ (company spend at or under €2.9 billion). ESG rating upgraded. Dividends maintained.

**Acceptable:** 6-8 years. Return 8.5%+ (company spend at or under €3.1 billion). Improving ESG. Credit rating maintained.

**Avoid:** Return below 7% (company spend above €3.3 billion). Unlimited costs. No accountability. Credit downgrade.

**Your no-deal outcome:** A decade of regulatory risk and stranded-asset write-downs. The ESG rating stays weak and the share price stagnates.

---

### NEGOTIATION TIPS

- **Your currency is the return rule.** Turn every demand into "how many return points does this cost?" Publicly funded packages cost you nothing; packages on company credit cost you.
- **Your signature is not required for a Deal Sheet,** but Management cannot spend above €3.1 billion without you, and the €750 million in new equity needs your vote. Use that.
- **Trade-offs to consider:** a cheap deal that collapses politically vs. a more expensive deal that brings stability; strict cost caps vs. ESG gains from a visible just transition.
- **Your vulnerability:** You look like the group that only cares about money. Show that you support fair packages, as long as someone other than the company pays for them or they stay within your return floor.

---

### USEFUL PHRASES

- "Fiduciary duty requires that the transition create value, not destroy it..."
- "Climate risk is financial risk. We need this transition done well..."
- "We support fair packages. Social license reduces risk. But who funds them?"
- "Returns below 8% are not acceptable. That's the minimum, not exceptional..."
