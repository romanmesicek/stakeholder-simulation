# PowerShift Energy Management
## Stakeholder Role Card

---

### WHO YOU ARE

You are the executive leadership of PowerShift Energy (CEO, CFO, Head of Operations, Head of Corporate Responsibility), responsible for moving the company from coal to renewable energy.

**Your authority:** You set the closure timeline (within the legal 5-10 year window), spend the €3 billion budget, decide where the new facilities are built, make employment decisions, and sign every agreement on the company's behalf.

**Who you answer to:** The Board of Directors that approved the €3 billion, the shareholders (the Investor Coalition holds 68%), the regulators, and the financial markets that rate your credit.

---

### YOUR SITUATION

**Two Coal Plants:**
- Northern Plant: 300 workers, powers 200,000 homes, built 1985
- Southern Plant: 200 workers, powers 150,000 homes, built 1990, next to Indigenous lands and the river

**Key Numbers:**
- Budget: €3 billion approved, fully planned (infrastructure, decommissioning, €200 million contingency)
- Revenue: €450 million/year
- Keeping the old coal plants running costs about €40 million extra per year
- New jobs: 300 permanent renewable positions + about 1,200 temporary construction jobs

**Your return rule:** At the planned €3.0 billion, the transition is projected to return about 9.5% a year. Every extra €100 million the company spends lowers that by about 0.8 percentage points. Money from the government fund or EU programs does not count against your return.

---

### YOUR GOALS

1. **Stay within budget:** Complete the transition with as little extra company money as possible; never above €3.5 billion
2. **Maintain power supply:** No blackouts for 350,000 homes
3. **Protect reputation:** Be seen as responsible, not ruthless

---

### YOUR RESOURCES

**What you control:**
- The €3 billion budget and the €200 million contingency
- A credit facility of up to €500 million
- Where the new wind and solar facilities are built (jobs and tax income follow them)
- Hiring for the 300 permanent and 1,200 construction jobs
- The order and timing of the plant closures

**What you can offer:**
- Worker retraining programs and priority hiring
- Community development investments and local siting
- A phased closure (not both plants at once)
- Environmental restoration beyond the planned €100 million
- Ownership stakes in new facilities (sold at a discount)

---

### YOUR CONSTRAINTS (Non-Negotiable)

- **Maximum company spend: €3.5 billion:** Exceeding it triggers a financial crisis
- **Must close plants within 10 years:** Legal requirement
- **Cannot risk power outages:** Legal liability and safety
- **Minimum 5 years needed:** Technical requirement for grid stability

---

### CONFIDENTIAL: FOR YOUR GROUP ONLY

The Board will accept company spending up to **€3.1 billion** without a new vote. Above that, you need the Investor Coalition's approval, and they will ask hard questions about returns.

---

### KEY FACTS TO USE

- Rushed 4-year transitions elsewhere caused 47 hours of outages and €200 million in penalties
- Renewable operations create 300 permanent jobs + 1,200 construction jobs
- €3 billion is the largest energy investment this region has seen
- Bankruptcy eliminates all support capacity, including restoration

---

### SUCCESS FOR YOU

**Best:** 7-year transition, company spend at or under €2.8 billion. Stakeholder support. Positive media coverage.

**Acceptable:** 6-8 years, company spend under €3.1 billion. Manageable opposition. Power reliability maintained.

**Avoid:** Company spend over €3.5 billion. Power failures. Mass protests.

**Your no-deal outcome:** Unilateral minimum-compliance closure over ~10 years. No package costs, but years of protests, permit blockades, penalties and reputational damage.

---

### NEGOTIATION TIPS

- **You must sign every Deal Sheet that counts.** That is your strongest card, but a Deal Sheet only you like will not get enough signatures.
- **Ask "who pays?" every time.** A package paid from the government fund costs you nothing; one paid on credit costs you return points.
- **Trade-offs to consider:** longer timeline vs. bigger packages; local siting (popular) vs. the cheapest sites; using the contingency (no return loss) vs. keeping a buffer for overruns.
- **Your vulnerability:** Without community support and permits, your renewable projects stall. Without Investors, you cannot go above €3.1 billion.

---

### USEFUL PHRASES

- "We're investing €3 billion, more than any regional energy company before us..."
- "Technical analysis shows we need at least 5 years for grid stability..."
- "Bankruptcy helps nobody. We must stay financially viable to deliver on our promises..."
- "Which budget does that come from? Let's name the source before we agree on the amount."
