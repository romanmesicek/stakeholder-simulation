# Coal Plant Workers Union
## Stakeholder Role Card

---

### WHO YOU ARE

You represent 500 coal plant workers and their 2,000 family members. Your members have worked here for decades and have no other local employment options. Many have mortgages and families depending on these jobs.

**Your authority:** Collective bargaining for all 500 workers (almost all are union members), the legal right to strike, and the operational knowledge without which the plants cannot be run or shut down safely. Any agreement that changes jobs or pensions needs a vote of your members.

**Who you answer to:** Your members and their families. They will vote on whatever you sign.

---

### YOUR SITUATION

**Worker Profile:**
- 500 workers across both plants (300 Northern, 200 Southern)
- Average tenure: 18 years; average age: 45
- Almost a quarter are over 55 and close to retirement
- Most have only coal plant experience
- Many have coal-related health conditions from years of work
- 90 of the 200 Southern Plant workers are Indigenous

**Financial Reality:**
- Monthly income: €2,500-4,500
- Average mortgage: €120,000
- Limited savings (about 6 months of expenses)

**The job math:** The transition creates 300 permanent jobs in renewable operations and about 1,200 temporary construction jobs. Not all 500 of your members can get a permanent job there.

---

### YOUR GOALS

1. **No forced unemployment:** Every worker needs a real alternative (a new job, paid retraining, or a fair exit package)
2. **Protect pensions:** Workers earned these over decades; bridge payments for older workers
3. **Preserve community:** Prevent economic collapse in Northern Town

---

### YOUR RESOURCES

**What you control:**
- Operational knowledge essential for safe plant operation and shutdown
- Legal right to strike (would halt power for 350,000 homes)
- Strong public sympathy
- Political connections (workers vote)
- Skills transferable to renewable operations

**What you can offer:**
- Cooperation with a phased closure
- Knowledge for safe decommissioning
- Workforce for renewable construction and operations
- No strikes if treated fairly

---

### YOUR CONSTRAINTS (Non-Negotiable)

- **No forced layoffs without fair packages:** Minimum 1.5 months' pay per year worked
- **Full pension protection:** No reductions
- **Minimum 5-6 year timeline:** Workers need time to retrain and adjust
- **Quality retraining:** Real programs with recognized qualifications

---

### KEY FACTS TO USE

- 500 jobs support 2,000 family members, and about 750 more jobs in the region depend on the plants
- Workers have €120,000 mortgages: unemployment means foreclosure
- Many workers have health conditions from decades of keeping the lights on
- Retraining takes 12-18 months: it must start long before the first closure

---

### SUCCESS FOR YOU

**Best:** €250-300 million worker fund. 70% get renewable jobs. 7-8 year timeline.

**Acceptable:** €200 million fund. 50% get jobs. 6-7 year timeline.

**Avoid:** Under €100 million support. Forced layoffs. Timeline under 5 years.

**Your no-deal outcome:** Statutory severance only: no fund, no retraining, no priority hiring, layoffs spread over an unmanaged 10-year decline.

---

### NEGOTIATION TIPS

- **Count the jobs.** 300 permanent jobs cannot absorb 500 workers. Combine jobs, retraining, early retirement for older workers, and exit packages into one offer.
- **Ask where the fund comes from.** A promise without a funding source is worth nothing on the Deal Sheet.
- **Trade-offs to consider:** a faster timeline in exchange for a bigger fund; guaranteed jobs vs. higher exit payments; your members' packages vs. community-wide investments.
- **Your vulnerability:** The strike threat is powerful but can turn public opinion against you. Keep it in the background.

---

### USEFUL PHRASES

- "500 workers support 2,000 family members. These aren't just jobs, they're livelihoods..."
- "We have the skills you need for renewable operations. Invest in retraining..."
- "Workers sacrificed their health for 40 years keeping the region's lights on..."
- "We're not against renewable energy. We just need fair treatment..."
