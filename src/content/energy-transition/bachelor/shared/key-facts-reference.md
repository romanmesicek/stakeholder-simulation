# Key Facts: Quick Reference

Facts every group can cite during negotiations. Your role card contains additional information only you know.

## The Company

| Plant | Capacity | Workers | Homes Served | Built |
|-------|----------|---------|--------------|-------|
| Northern Plant | 500 MW | 300 | 200,000 | 1985 |
| Southern Plant | 300 MW | 200 | 150,000 | 1990 |

- Total: 800 MW, 500 workers, 350,000 households served
- Annual revenue: €450 million
- Annual CO₂ emissions: 4 million tons (35% of the region's emissions)
- Tax contribution: €8 million/year (€5 million municipal + €3 million regional)

## Jobs

- Today: 500 direct plant jobs, plus about 750 indirect jobs in the region (suppliers, shops, services)
- After the transition: 300 permanent jobs in renewable operations
- During the build-out: about 1,200 construction jobs, temporary (they end when the facilities are finished)

## The Places

- **Northern Town:** 15,000 residents; the plant provides 40% of local jobs and its taxes fund 55% of the schools
- **Southern Region:** 8,000 residents, rural; the plant provides 25% of jobs
- **Indigenous territory:** about 3,000 Indigenous people live on traditional lands around the Southern Plant; 90 of the plant's 200 workers are Indigenous. The river that cools the plant runs through their territory; mercury in its fish is 300% above safe levels. The constitution recognizes their rights, including consultation on development on their land
- Some of the best wind and solar sites lie on Indigenous territory and around Northern Town

## The Money

- Company investment: €3 billion (approved by the Board)
- Government transition fund: €500 million
- **Total available: €3.5 billion**
- Planned renewables: 450 MW wind (3 farms) + 350 MW solar (5 sites) = 800 MW

**How the €3 billion is planned:**

| Category | Estimated Cost |
|----------|----------------|
| Renewable infrastructure (wind + solar) | €1.8 billion |
| Grid upgrades and storage | €600 million |
| Plant decommissioning and site restoration | €400 million |
| *of which safe shutdown and demolition* | *about €300 million* |
| *of which site restoration (soil, water)* | *about €100 million* |
| Contingency (buffer for cost overruns) | €200 million |
| **Total company budget** | **€3.0 billion** |

Large infrastructure projects run on average 25% over budget. The contingency exists for that reason.

**Where can money for stakeholder packages come from?**

Worker and community packages are **not** a line item in the €3 billion plan. Neither is restoration beyond the planned €100 million, nor an ownership stake for the Indigenous Community. Possible sources:

- **Government transition fund:** €500 million, decided by the Regional Government
- **Contingency:** €200 million, if the company agrees to commit it (it then no longer covers cost overruns)
- **Credit facility:** up to €500 million of new borrowing, the headroom between €3.0 and €3.5 billion; this raises the company's spending
- **Reallocation** within the plan, e.g. moving money between categories

Every euro promised must come from somewhere. Ask which source funds each commitment.

**Ownership stakes:** The company can sell the Indigenous Community a share of the facilities built on its territory at a discount, so the community earns from them for decades. Each 10% stake costs about €30 million (the discount); someone has to pay it.

## The Timeline

| Project Type | Development Time |
|--------------|------------------|
| Wind farm | 24-30 months |
| Solar installation | 18-24 months |
| Battery storage | 18-24 months |

- Eight facilities cannot all be built at once
- Realistic full transition: **6-7 years**
- Below 5 years: serious risk of power outages for 350,000 homes
- The law requires both plants to close within 10 years at the latest

## Law & Climate Targets

- EU target: 55% emissions reduction by 2030
- Binding regional requirement: 45% reduction by 2030
- Missing the regional target costs €50 million in penalties per year, paid from public budgets

## Costs of Electricity (per MWh)

- Solar and wind: €25-45
- Coal: €65-150 (including environmental costs)

## Glossary

- **MW / MWh:** Megawatt is the capacity of a plant (how much power it can deliver at once); megawatt-hour is the amount of electricity produced
- **Contingency:** money set aside for overruns and surprises
- **Credit facility:** a pre-agreed loan the company can draw on
- **Stranded asset:** an investment that loses its value early, e.g. a coal plant that must close before it has paid off
- **Return / hurdle rate:** the yearly profit an investment earns, in percent; the hurdle rate is the minimum investors accept
- **Credit rating:** a grade from rating agencies on how safe it is to lend to a company; a downgrade makes borrowing more expensive
- **ESG:** environmental, social and governance performance, which investors use to judge how responsibly a company is run
- **Just transition:** a shift to clean energy that does not leave workers and communities to carry the costs alone
- **No-deal outcome (BATNA):** what happens to your group if no agreement is reached; BATNA stands for "best alternative to a negotiated agreement". Never accept a deal that is worse for you than this
- **Deal Sheet:** the written agreement that counts as the conference outcome (see the Student Guide)
- **Feasibility Certificate:** the Technical Expert Panel's sign-off on a Deal Sheet; needed only if the Panel takes part
