# Energy Transition Simulation: Student Guide

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this simulation you will be able to:

- Analyze a multi-stakeholder conflict in terms of interests, positions, and power
- Negotiate within real constraints and judge offers against your walk-away alternative (your no-deal outcome)
- Explain how coalitions form, hold, and break, and what membership costs
- Connect the trade-offs of a just energy transition (jobs, climate, cost, reliability) to real-world cases

**Preparation:** Read the situation briefing *before* the session. In-session reading time only covers your role card and the Key Facts.

## Phase 0: Opening & Role Distribution

- Draw a role slip randomly
- Join your stakeholder group table
- Collect your materials packet

---

## Phase 1: Reading & Strategy

1. **Read** your role card and the Key Facts sheet
2. **Understand** your goals, resources, and constraints
3. **Work through the strategy questions** below with your group
4. **Prepare** a 1-minute opening statement

Your role card is confidential. Do not share your specific constraints or bottom lines.

**Strategy questions** (write down short answers):

1. **Walk-away line:** What is our no-deal outcome? Which offers are worse than that?
2. **Top two demands:** What do we need most, in numbers (years, €, %, jobs)?
3. **What we can give:** Which of our resources do others need? What does giving it cost us?
4. **Who holds the key:** Which groups control the money, permits or signatures we need?
5. **First offer:** Where do we open, and where do we stop? Open above your Acceptable line, never below it.

---

## Phase 2: Pitches & Voting

The facilitator chairs this phase in the side-role of **Regional Energy Minister**, who has convened the transition conference.

**Opening Statements:** Each group presents for 1 minute, in this order (skip groups that are not in play):

1. PowerShift Energy Management
2. Coal Plant Workers Union
3. Local Community Coalition
4. Environmental Alliance
5. Regional Government
6. Indigenous Community
7. Investor Coalition
8. Technical Expert Panel

**Voting** via Mentimeter:
- Rate each stakeholder on Power (1–10)
- Predict the outcome: Agreement / Partial / Deadlock
- Pre-questions (revisited after the simulation):
  - What is a realistic timeline for this coal exit? (years)
  - Who should carry most of the transition's cost? (company / government / consumers)
  - How likely is an agreement today? (1–10)

---

## Phase 3: Negotiations

| Round | Format |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | Assigned bilateral: meet your assigned partner |
| 2 | Open bilateral: choose a group you have not met yet |
| 3 | Coalitions & Deal Sheet: form groups of 3–4, write and sign a Deal Sheet |

**Round 1 pairings:**

| Groups in play | Pairings |
|----------------|----------|
| 6 | Management + Workers · Community + Government · Environmental + Investors |
| 7 (with Indigenous or Technical) | Management + Workers · Community + Government · Environmental + Investors + the seventh group |
| 8 | Management + Workers · Community + Government · Environmental + Indigenous · Investors + Technical |

**Round 2:** Choose strategically: meet a group that could strengthen or threaten your plans.

**Round 3:** Form coalitions, negotiate trade-offs, and put your agreement on a Deal Sheet.

**Remember:** No agreement is a real outcome. Your constraints are real.

---

## The Deal Sheet

An agreement only counts if it is written down. One page, with:

- **Timeline:** closure year for each plant, which plant closes first
- **Packages,** each with an amount in € **and** its funding source (government fund, contingency, credit facility, reallocation):
  - Worker support (fund, retraining, renewable jobs)
  - Community fund and tax replacement; share of renewables sited locally
  - Restoration (plant sites, and the river if the Indigenous Community is in play)
  - Indigenous ownership stake and jobs *(if in play)*
- **What each signing group gives in return** (e.g. no strikes, sites, no legal challenge, capital approval)
- **Signatures** of the groups that agree

**The agreement rule:** A Deal Sheet becomes the conference outcome if it is signed by **Management**, the **Regional Government**, and **at least half of the other groups** in play. If the Technical Expert Panel plays, the Deal Sheet also needs its **Feasibility Certificate**. Management and the Government can each sign only one Deal Sheet.

---

## Phase 4: Final Conference & Results

The **Regional Energy Minister** (facilitator) chairs the closing conference: opens it, moderates the statements, and announces the outcome.

- Coalitions present their Deal Sheet(s)
- Each group gives a 1-minute final statement: do we sign, and why (not)?
- If the Technical Expert Panel is in play: it grants or refuses the Feasibility Certificate; if it refuses, it reads out a public risk statement
- The Minister announces whether a Deal Sheet meets the agreement rule; if none does, the no-deal default applies
- Voting results revealed: compare predictions with reality
- Post-round of the pre-questions (timeline, who pays, likelihood): discuss what shifted and why

---

## Phase 5: Debrief

Led by the facilitator, out of role:

1. **How are you feeling?** Step out of your role. First reactions.
2. **What happened?** Which coalitions formed, and why? What were the turning points? Where did power decide?
3. **What did you learn?** Compare the outcome with each group's Best/Acceptable/Avoid ladder and no-deal outcome. What made offers credible?
4. **Real-world transfer:** Where does this conflict play out in reality, and what would help there?

See the Debriefing Guide for detailed questions per step.

**Reflection Assignment: Due [Date TBD]**

800–1000 words addressing:
1. **Experience:** What was the most significant moment?
2. **Analysis:** How did power dynamics shape the outcome?
3. **Learning:** What did you learn about stakeholder negotiations?

## Staying in Role: Ground Rules

1. **Stay in character** during all negotiations: speak as "we", not "I think"
2. **Your constraints are real:** honor them
3. **No agreement is a valid outcome:** don't force consensus
4. **Attack positions, not people:** disagreement is expected and valuable
5. **Listen as much as you advocate,** take notes on offers, and look for creative trade-offs
