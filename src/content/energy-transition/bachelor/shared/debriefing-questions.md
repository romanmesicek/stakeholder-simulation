# Debriefing Guide

A guide for facilitating the post-simulation discussion. Give the most time to Phases 2 and 3; keep Phase 1 short but don't skip it.

---

## Phase 1: How Are You Feeling?

Let participants decompress after their roles.

- How are you feeling right now?
- What was the most frustrating moment?
- What was the most satisfying moment?

---

## Phase 2: What Happened?

Surface observations before analysis.

**The result:**
- Put the final Deal Sheet (or the no-deal default) on the board. Next to it, each group places itself on its own ladder: Best, Acceptable, Avoid, or worse than no-deal?
- Who moved most from their opening position? Who moved least, and why could they afford to?
- Which promises have a named funding source, and which don't?

**Alliances & Conflicts:**
- What alliances formed? What enabled them?
- Which groups ended up isolated? Why?

**Power Dynamics:**
- Who had the most influence? Why?
- Did any group's power change during the simulation?

**Negotiations:**
- What creative solutions emerged?
- What blocked potential agreements?

---

## Phase 3: What Did You Learn?

Connect to course concepts.

- What made finding a solution so difficult?
- Could any single stakeholder have solved this alone?
- Which stakeholders' needs were hardest to reconcile?
- How did technical constraints affect what was possible?

**Theory anchors:** one minute of input each, then the question.

- **BATNA / reservation point:** Each card's Best/Acceptable/Avoid ladder plus its no-deal outcome is a walk-away alternative in miniature: it defines which offers a rational negotiator can accept. *Who knew their walk-away line, and who negotiated without one?*
- **Power vs. interest:** Power (ability to shape the outcome) and interest (how strongly a group is affected) often diverge. *Map the groups in play on both axes: did the most affected have the most say?*
- **Just transition in three questions:** *Who pays?* (distribution of costs and benefits) · *Who decides?* (whose signature counted, who was only consulted) · *Who is heard?* (whose identity and needs were taken seriously). *Answer all three for your outcome. Where is it fair, where not?*

---

## Phase 4: Real-World Application

- How might real energy transitions differ from this simulation?
- Real cases to compare: Germany's coal commission (2019) brought unions, industry, environmental groups, scientists and the coal regions to one table and recommended a coal exit by 2038 at the latest, with about €40 billion in structural aid for the regions. Spain's 2018 agreement for its coal-mining regions was negotiated between government, unions and companies. *What did these processes do that your conference did or didn't do?*
- What surprised you about your own behavior in the role?

---

## Facilitator Tips

**Use your notes:** Replay one or two verbatim quotes from the observation sheet; concrete moments open the discussion faster than general questions.

**Common Pitfalls:**
- Skipping the feelings phase
- Letting one voice dominate
- Providing "correct answers" instead of facilitating discussion

**Signs of Success:**
- Participants reference specific moments
- "Aha" moments about power dynamics
- Curiosity about other groups' experiences
