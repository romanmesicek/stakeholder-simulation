# Key Facts & Reference

The shared fact base every group can cite during negotiations. Role cards contain additional confidential information.

**Reliability flags, read this first.** This sheet works like a real evidence base, which means it is not clean:

- **[CONFLICTING]**: credible sources disagree; both figures are quotable
- **[INDUSTRY SOURCE]**: self-reported by companies or commissioned by them, not independently audited
- **[NGO SOURCE]**: modeled by an advocacy organization; methodology disclosed, not company-confirmed
- **[PROJECTION]**: a forecast, not a measurement

Unflagged figures are independently verified. Quoting a flagged figure is legitimate, but expect to be challenged on it, and challenge others when they lean on one.

How to read this: the crisis is real and geographically concentrated at the same time. Ireland's numbers are government-verified; the EU-wide average is unremarkable. Both halves of that sentence are quotable.

## 4. Water: What Happened Elsewhere

| Case | What happened |
|---|---|
| Middenmeer (NL) | A hyperscale site consumed 84 M liters of drinking water in drought-year 2021, which was 4–7× the projected 12–20 M L/yr [CONFLICTING gross vs. net; partly one-off construction use] |
| Aragón (ES) | Licensed 755,720 m³/yr; the operator requested a 48% increase in 2024, independently documented; campaign "Your Cloud Is Drying My River" |
| Santiago de Chile | An environmental court temporarily revoked a project's authorization over aquifer concerns; the company paused and redesigned the cooling system |
| Global siting pattern | More than two-thirds of data centers built since 2022 are in water-stressed regions |

Modern closed-loop designs cut water use by an order of magnitude. The criticism is solid for specific water-stressed places and specific designs, and overgeneralized as a claim about the category.

## 6. Waste Heat

| Fact | Figure |
|---|---|
| Physics | Air-cooled campuses reject heat at 30–40 °C; legacy district heating needs 70–80 °C, so heat pumps are required |
| Economics | Pipeline economics deteriorate sharply beyond ~3–4 km to an existing network (Falkenberg: 3.2 km) |
| Delivered, not promised | Dublin/Tallaght: 92% of a university campus's heat from a data center; Espoo (FI): ~250,000 residents, ~40% of district-heat demand; Odense (DK): operational at scale |
| Why the successes worked | Pre-existing utility-owned networks, and a utility that wanted decarbonized heat: the heat pump and network investment was the utility's business case, not a developer gift |
| The failure pattern | Heat offered "free at the fence", nobody funds pipes and heat pumps, the offer dies (the Zeewolde lesson) |
| EU law | Facilities >1 MW must perform a waste-heat cost-benefit analysis and use the heat unless infeasible. This is an analysis obligation, not a funding obligation |

No project in this record was stopped by a referendum. They were stopped by elections, courts, land control, and connection policy, and saved by conditions.

## Glossary

- **Hyperscaler**: one of the handful of global cloud and AI companies (think Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta) operating data centers at 100+ MW scale
- **PUE** (Power Usage Effectiveness): total facility energy divided by IT energy; 1.0 is perfect, the industry average is ~1.55, the best campuses reach ~1.1
- **WUE** (Water Usage Effectiveness): liters of water per kWh of IT energy
- **Closed-loop cooling**: recirculating liquid cooling; near-zero water consumption, higher capital cost
- **District heating**: a network distributing heat to buildings from central sources; the precondition for waste-heat reuse
- **PPA** (Power Purchase Agreement): a long-term contract to buy electricity from (often new) renewable plants; "additionality" asks whether the contract caused new capacity to be built
- **24/7 matching**: matching consumption with carbon-free power every hour on the local grid, in contrast to the looser annual matching behind most "100% renewable" claims
- **EIA**: environmental impact assessment, with formal public participation
- **BATNA**: best alternative to a negotiated agreement, in other words your walk-away option
- **ZOPA**: zone of possible agreement, the range where a deal is possible at all
