# The Falkenberg Data Center — Student Guide

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this simulation you will be able to:

- Negotiate a facility-siting decision using the European instrument set — binding permit conditions, connection policy, procedural rights — and explain how it differs from cash-for-acceptance deals
- Argue with contested evidence: deploy flagged figures strategically, challenge others' numbers, and survive challenges to your own
- Build coalitions under an explicit approval threshold, and account for what membership costs each party
- Judge every offer against your no-deal alternative (BATNA) — and recognize when walking away is rational
- Apply social-license-to-operate and stakeholder-salience concepts to a live infrastructure conflict

**Preparation (homework, before the session):** Read the situation briefing *and* the Key Facts & Reference sheet. In-session reading time covers only your role card.

## The Agreement Rule

The negotiation succeeds only if a **Falkenberg Accord** is concluded, and an accord stands only if:

1. The **State Permitting Authority** accepts the conditions package as legally defensible
2. The **Regional Grid Operator** confirms connection feasibility under the agreed cost allocation
3. At least **4 of the 5** civil parties endorse it: Municipal Council, Citizens' Initiative, GreenGrid Europe, Development Agency, Farmers & Landowners
4. **NimbusData** concludes that the package still beats its alternative site

Read the arithmetic: the two technical gates are absolute, and among the five civil parties only *one* can be left behind. Nobody at this table can be ignored twice. **No agreement is a real outcome** — the briefing's no-deal default then applies, and for some parties that may genuinely be the better result.

## Phase 0: Opening & Role Distribution (5 min)

- Join your stakeholder group
- Collect your materials; your role card is **confidential**
- Within your group, assign sub-roles: **lead negotiator**, **analyst** (owns your numbers and the Key Facts flags), **note-taker** (documents every offer)

## Phase 1: Reading & Strategy (35 min)

1. **Read** your role card carefully (15 min)
2. **Locate** yourself: What can you block? What can you offer? What is your no-deal outcome?
3. **Check the Key Facts sheet** for figures that help you — and figures that will be used against you; prepare responses
4. **Strategize:** Whose endorsement do you need? Who needs yours? What is your opening ask, and what is the minimum you can defend at home?
5. **Prepare** a 1-minute opening statement anchored in your legitimate claims

## Phase 2: Opening Statements & Stakeholder Mapping (20 min)

The facilitator chairs the session in the side-role of **State Secretary for Digital Infrastructure**, who has convened the Falkenberg Siting Conference.

**Opening statements (10 min)** — 1 minute per group, in order: NimbusData · Municipal Council · Citizens' Initiative · GreenGrid Europe · Grid Operator · Development Agency · Farmers & Landowners · State Permitting Authority

**Stakeholder mapping (10 min)** — via Mentimeter:
- Rate each stakeholder on Interest (1–10) and Power (1–10)
- Predict the outcome: Accord with strong conditions / Accord with weak conditions / No accord — project leaves / No accord — project proceeds anyway
- Pre-questions (revisited after the simulation):
  - How many permanent jobs will this campus realistically create?
  - Which constraint will bind hardest? (grid capacity / water / land ownership / public trust)
  - Who should pay for the grid line and the heat network — the developer, customers, or taxpayers?

## Phase 3: Negotiation Round 1 — Bilaterals (45 min)

| Round | Duration | Format |
|-------|----------|--------|
| 1a | 12 min | Assigned bilateral — meet your designated partner |
| 1b | 12 min | Assigned bilateral — meet your second designated partner |
| 1c | 12 min | Open bilateral — choose a group you haven't met |
| 1d | 9 min | Group huddle — consolidate what you learned, adjust strategy |

**Round 1a pairings:** NimbusData + Municipal Council | Citizens' Initiative + GreenGrid | Farmers + Development Agency | Grid Operator + Permitting Authority

**Round 1b pairings:** NimbusData + Farmers | Municipal Council + Grid Operator | Citizens' Initiative + Permitting Authority | GreenGrid + Development Agency

**Round 1c:** Choose strategically — meet the group that can make or break your coalition.

## Phase 4: Caucus Break (15 min)

Groups caucus internally; coalitions may meet. The State Secretary may deliver **breaking news** that changes someone's leverage. Re-check your red lines before Round 2 — under time pressure is when teams betray their own instructions.

## Phase 5: Negotiation Round 2 — Coalitions & Drafting (45 min)

- Form negotiation clusters around the four strands: **water regime**, **heat deal**, **grid & Phase 2**, **jobs & transparency**
- Draft concrete accord language: numbers, funding sources, enforcement — "who pays how much for what, verified by whom"
- Every commitment needs a named funding source; the facilitator will strike unfunded promises from the record
- Document everything in writing; oral agreements do not reach the conference floor

## Phase 6: The Falkenberg Siting Conference (30 min)

The State Secretary chairs the closing conference:

- Each group: 2-minute final statement — what you endorse, what you refuse, and why
- The draft accord (or competing drafts) is read out strand by strand
- The Permitting Authority and Grid Operator declare their gates passed or failed, with reasons
- The five civil parties declare endorsement — the 4-of-5 threshold is counted publicly
- NimbusData declares: sign, or activate the alternative site
- Outcome summary; Mentimeter post-round — compare predictions and pre-question answers with reality

## Phase 7: Structured Debrief (25 min)

Follows the debriefing guide: defusing → discovery → deepening → transfer. This block is protected — it is where the learning consolidates.

**Reflection Assignment — Due: [Date TBD]**

1000–1200 words addressing:
1. **Experience** — Most significant moment? What surprised you?
2. **Analysis** — How did stakeholder salience (power, legitimacy, urgency) shift — and what shifted it?
3. **Evidence** — Which contested figure mattered most, how was it used, and how was it challenged?
4. **Theory** — Connect your observations to social license, BATNA/ZOPA, and regulatory conditionality vs. negotiated benefits
5. **Transfer** — Compare your outcome with a real case from the briefing (Kronstorf, Dublin, Zeewolde, Espoo): what does the comparison reveal?

## Staying in Role — Ground Rules

1. **Stay in character** during all negotiations — speak as "we", not "I think"
2. **Your constraints are real** — honor them, even when a deal is tantalizingly close
3. **No agreement is a valid outcome** — don't force consensus
4. **Attack positions and numbers, not people** — disagreement is expected and valuable
5. **Cite your sources** — a figure from the Key Facts sheet beats a figure from nowhere, and a flagged figure invites the challenge it deserves
