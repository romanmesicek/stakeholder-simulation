# Farmers & Landowners
## Stakeholder Role Card

---

### WHO YOU ARE

You are the negotiating delegation of the Verla Valley farming community: **Martin Brandt** (whose family owns 9 hectares inside the zoned site), **Theresa Keller** (whose family owns the other 6 hectares — and whose farmhouse stands 300 meters from the planned halls), and the chair of the **irrigation association**, which holds the valley's senior water rights. Your families have farmed this floodplain for generations. Fifteen years ago, when the land was zoned industrial, your parents held out. Now the question is back, ten times bigger, and this time the answer is complicated — because it is not the same answer for each of you.

**Your authority:** Ownership of the 15 hectares without which Project Aurora shrinks by a third and loses its future — and the irrigation association's senior rights of **450,000 m³/year** from the Verla.

**Who you answer to:** Your own families, the association's members along the river, and neighbors who will remember what you did here for the next forty years.

---

### YOUR SITUATION

**The land — where your delegation is split:**
- **Brandt** wants to sell. The offered €45/m² would turn 9 hectares into ~€4 million — transformative money for a farm that has been living on margins
- **Keller** wants to stay. No price relocates three generations, a herd, and a farmhouse that would face a 20-meter data hall across the field. Her questions are noise, light, truck traffic, and what happens to her well
- You have agreed to negotiate **as one delegation** — because the moment you split, everyone else gets your land cheaper and your water for free

**The water — where you are united:**
- The association's 450,000 m³/year of senior rights predate every industrial permit in the valley
- NimbusData's filing wants up to 600,000 m³/year from the same river that irrigates your fields — and the filing says nothing about dry years
- Seniority on paper is worth exactly as much as its enforcement in a drought. You want it *written into the new permit*: in scarcity, irrigation and the river come first

**The prices — your benchmark file:**
- SMEs in the same zone paid €70/m²; you are offered €45
- Elsewhere in Europe, a below-market land deal for a hyperscaler ("€70 vs. €125 for local firms") became a national scandal that helped kill the project — you have that newspaper clipping in the folder

---

### YOUR PRIMARY GOALS

**1. Water Seniority, Written and Enforceable**
- The new water permit must rank irrigation rights and river ecology above data-center cooling in scarcity — a drought clause with automatic curtailment, not a goodwill letter
- Independent metering and published withdrawal data — you want to *see* the numbers, not request them

**2. A Fair Deal for Both Families — or No Deal on the Land**
- For the sellers: €55/m² or better (the SME benchmark is €70 — you are being modest and you'll say so), plus payment terms that survive tax advice
- For the Kellers: a genuine choice — either a premium price *if* she chooses to sell, or bindingly: a 150-meter buffer, an earth berm with planting, noise limits with low-frequency specifications, truck routing away from her lane, and a guaranteed well
- The delegation signs together or not at all

**3. Infrastructure That Outlives the Hype**
- The €5 million reused-water irrigation reservoir: drought insurance for the whole valley, fed by the campus's treated outflow — your version of the waste-heat deal
- Access: field roads, drainage, and fencing restored and maintained at the developer's cost

---

### YOUR RESOURCES

**What you can offer:**
- **The 15 hectares** — the difference between an 80 MW consolation prize and the 450 MW future NimbusData actually wants; nobody else at this table owns anything the developer needs this badly
- **Your endorsement** — the most credible signature in the region: when farming families say "the water is safe," the pragmatic middle believes it in a way no press release achieves
- **Association party status** — like the citizens' initiative, the irrigation association has formal standing in the water-permit hearing

**What you can deploy:**
- The photograph: tractors in front of the town hall, three generations of Kellers at the fence line — the most powerful image in the conflict, usable exactly once
- The clipping file: land-price scandals, drought-year water grabs, the court that suspended a project over an aquifer

---

### YOUR CONSTRAINTS (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

**The Delegation Does Not Split**
Why: united you are indispensable; divided you are two small landowners and a water club. Consequence: no separate deals, whatever is slid across the table — and something will be.

**Seniority Is Not Traded for Money**
Why: water rights sold once are gone forever, and the association's members would never ratify it. Consequence: cash can buy land and buffers; it cannot buy the drought clause away.

**No Expropriation Shadow**
Why: the moment anyone hints at compulsory purchase, negotiation is over. Consequence: any such hint and the delegation walks to the hearing room — publicly.

**Keller's Choice Stays Hers**
Why: the delegation's honor and the valley's cohesion. Consequence: Brandt does not pressure Keller, and neither does anyone else through Brandt.

---

### KEY FACTS TO USE

- 450,000 m³/year of senior rights vs. a 600,000 m³/year filing on the same river — the arithmetic of a dry summer, and the filing has no drought clause
- €45 offered vs. €70 paid by SMEs in the same zone — and the European precedent where exactly this asymmetry became a project-killing scandal
- One hyperscale site drank 84 million liters of *drinking water* in a drought year — 4–7× its projection; projections are not promises
- A company elsewhere requested 48% more water *after* approval — the reason you want caps and curtailment in the permit, not in a press release
- Without your 15 hectares: 80 MW, no Phase 2 — worth repeating slowly whenever the price discussion stalls

---

### CONFIDENTIAL — FOR YOUR GROUP ONLY

- **Brandt's clock:** the farm carries **€1.1 million of debt** from the new barn, and the bank has signaled it will not extend past next year. Martin needs a sale **within 12 months** — this one, realistically. If the project dies, so does the farm's refinancing. Nobody outside the family knows; if NimbusData learns it, your price collapses.
- **Keller's true line:** Theresa has privately decided she *would* sell — at **€90/m²** (≈€5.4 million for 6 ha), enough to buy the Hofer place upriver and move the whole operation. She has told only you. Played right, this is the endgame's decisive card: NimbusData gets its full site, Keller gets a future, and the "premium for the family that stays" narrative becomes "generous deal" instead of "displacement." Played wrong — revealed early — it reprices the whole negotiation against you.
- **The association's quiet worry:** enforcement of seniority in a real drought has never been tested in this valley; your lawyer thinks the current rights would hold but calls it "not risk-free." One more reason you want the ranking restated in the *new* permit — it converts your strongest claim from probable to explicit.

---

### WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE FOR YOU

**Best outcome:** Drought clause with automatic curtailment and published metering in the permit; €55+/m² for Brandt; Keller's choice honored at her price or full protections; the €5 million reservoir built; your endorsement delivered as the accord's credibility anchor.

**Acceptable outcome:** Drought clause and independent metering; €50/m² with terms; binding buffer/berm/noise/well package for Keller; reservoir committed with a named budget and date.

**Failure to avoid:** The delegation split by a side deal; seniority left to old paper and goodwill; an expropriation shadow accepted in silence; Brandt's distress or Keller's price leaking.

**Your no-deal outcome:** Keller keeps her farm and her uncertainty; the campus shrinks to 80 MW or the developer walks; Brandt faces the bank without a buyer; the association fights the water permit from the hearing room. Genuinely survivable for the valley — genuinely dangerous for one of you. That asymmetry is your secret; carry it carefully.

---

### USEFUL PHRASES FOR NEGOTIATION

- "Our rights are older than your project. We're not asking you to respect that — we're asking you to write it down."
- "The SMEs paid seventy. You're offering the people you need most forty-five. Explain that to us slowly."
- "What happens in the dry August? Show us the clause. If there is no clause, that *is* the answer."
- "You're not buying fifteen hectares — you're buying the difference between eighty megawatts and your entire plan."
- "When we say the water is safe, this valley believes it. That sentence has a price, and it's not just money."

---

### COALITION DYNAMICS

**Natural Allies:**
- **Citizens' Initiative**: the water coalition — their crowds and files, your rights and standing. Mind the gap: some of their members oppose *any* sale; don't let their fight cancel Brandt's future.
- **Permitting Authority**: the drought clause is *their* pen on *your* demand — deliver them the association's hydrological records and make the condition easy to draft.

**Potential Partners (with effort):**
- **NimbusData**: the counterpart, not the enemy — they need your land more than you need their money (only you know it's close). The reservoir and the metering cost them little and buy your headline sentence.
- **Municipal Council**: your endorsement plays exactly with the voters the mayor is losing; in return, the town should carry your access-and-roads list.
- **Development Agency**: holds the option contracts and fears you publicly; a fair-price settlement flips them from adversary to advocate overnight.

**Likely Adversaries:**
- Nobody by necessity — but everyone becomes one if the delegation splits. Your adversary is the *side deal*.

**Strategic Insight:** You hold the only asset in the game that cannot be substituted, regulated, or waited out — and the credibility that makes an accord believable to the people who live here. In the 4-of-5 arithmetic you are, with the initiative, the endorsement that means something. Sell land once, water never, and the signature last.

---

### STRATEGIC CONSIDERATIONS

**Your position strength:**
- Indispensable land, senior water rights, hearing standing, and the region's most trusted voice on the only question that decides elections here

**Your vulnerabilities:**
- Brandt's debt clock ticks against your patience strategy; Keller's secret price is a one-shot card; a split ends everything

**Key trade-offs:**
- Early land deal (secures Brandt, spends leverage) vs. package deal at the end (maximum price, maximum risk)
- The tractor photo now (pressure) vs. held back (deterrence) — it works once

**Negotiation strategy:**
1. Lead with water, not land — the drought clause unites your delegation and your allies; the price divides the room
2. Build the water axis early: Initiative + Association + Regulator, with the hydrological file on the table
3. Keep land talks packaged: price, terms, buffers, reservoir, roads — one envelope, one signature
4. Let NimbusData discover slowly that the full site is *possible* — Keller's card enters only when everything else is agreed
5. Time your endorsement for the conference floor: it is the accord's closing argument, and it should look like one
