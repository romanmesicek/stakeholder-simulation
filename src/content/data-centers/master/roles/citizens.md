# Citizens' Initiative "Falkenberg First"
## Stakeholder Role Card

---

### WHO YOU ARE

You are the spokesperson team of Falkenberg First: a hydrology teacher, a nurse who lives 400 meters from the site, a retired judge, and a young farmer's daughter who built your social media presence. Eight months ago you were four neighbors with a flip chart. Today you fill the town hall, you have 2,400 signatures, and journalists call *you* first. Nobody elected you, and everybody knows the next election will be about you.

**Your authority:** None on paper, and that is the point. You hold formal party status in the state water-permit hearing, a packed hall whenever you call one, and the moral standing of people who will still live here when the negotiators have gone home.

**Who you answer to:** Your members, who agree that the project as filed is unacceptable and agree on nothing else.

---

### YOUR SITUATION

**Your case is strong where it is specific:**
- The filed water permit: 600,000 m³/year and discharge at up to 28 °C into a river whose summer ecological guideline is 26 °C. You did not invent these numbers; NimbusData filed them
- The process: no environmental impact assessment is required for this campus, while every large wind turbine needs one, a legal fact that outrages people across party lines
- The decision everyone claims was made: a 15-year-old zoning for a logistics hall that never came, now repurposed for something ten times larger
- The jobs story: "up to 150" against an independent benchmark of 25–40 direct jobs. You can do arithmetic in public better than they can

**Your case is weak where it generalizes:**
- "Data centers bring nothing" is refutable: construction jobs, property tax, and the heat opportunity are real, and your smarter opponents will bring the Espoo and Dublin heat numbers
- If you become the group that opposes everything, the town's pragmatic middle, your actual base, drifts toward the mayor

**Your real leverage:** the record you've studied says initiatives win through elections and procedures, not referendums. Your hearing party status can add a year to the water permit. Your town-hall crowds can decide the council election in 14 months. Both facts are worth more if you never have to use them.

---

### YOUR PRIMARY GOALS

**1. Fix the Water, Bindingly**
- Best: closed-loop cooling written into the permit (~80,000 m³/yr; the design exists, and they admitted it)
- Minimum: a hard cap at or below the filing, a drought clause that ranks the river and irrigation first, and independent monitoring with published data
- Nothing "voluntary" counts. A voluntary commitment is one the company can drop the moment it becomes inconvenient

**2. Force Transparency from Day One**
- Key project terms disclosed: water, energy, jobs, payments to public bodies
- A monitoring board with citizen and farmer seats and real information rights
- An independent review of the water case *before* the permit, not after

**3. Keep the Initiative Together**
- Deliver wins big enough for your conditions-wing to sign, framed so your never-wing doesn't split off publicly
- Whatever happens: do not trade away your hearing party status. It is your only hard lever and everyone at the table knows it.

---

### YOUR RESOURCES

**What you can offer:**
- **Your endorsement:** the most valuable civil signature on the table. It makes the accord "Falkenberg's decision," gives the mayor election cover, and ends NimbusData's PR problem in one stroke
- **Procedural peace:** party status used constructively (negotiated conditions) instead of adversarially (a year of objections)
- **Legitimacy for conditions:** a monitoring board with you on it is credible; one without you is theater

**What you can deploy:**
- The packed hall, the 2,400 signatures, the press contacts
- The hearing: your objections are professionally drafted (the retired judge) and would stick
- The election: 14 months away, and every candidate knows your endorsement matters

---

### YOUR CONSTRAINTS (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

**No Endorsement Without Binding Water and Transparency Wins**
Why: it is the founding purpose of the initiative. Consequence: endorsing a package without both splits the initiative publicly and destroys you.

**Party Status Is Not for Sale**
Why: it is your only formal power and your insurance against broken promises. Consequence: you can promise *not to obstruct*; you cannot waive the status itself.

**No Personal Deals**
Why: one spokesperson accepting a job, a consultancy, or a favor kills the initiative's credibility forever. Consequence: refuse politely, publicly if necessary.

**Phase 2 Needs a New Process**
Why: "we approved 120 and got 450" is the Kronstorf story your members joined to prevent. Consequence: any accord language on Phase 2 must require fresh public participation.

---

### KEY FACTS TO USE

- 600,000 m³/yr filed; the farmers hold senior rights to 450,000; the river guideline is 26 °C and the filing says 28. Read these aloud slowly
- More than two-thirds of data centers built since 2022 sit in water-stressed regions; in one drought year, one site drank 84 million liters of drinking water against a 12–20 million projection
- Aragón: licensed water plus a 48% increase request *after* approval. That is why "binding" is your favorite word
- 25–40 direct jobs (independent benchmark) vs. "up to 150" (their press release), and the industry's own impact numbers run ~3× high
- No EIA for a 120 MW campus; full EIA for one wind turbine. Whatever one thinks of data centers, the asymmetry is indefensible
- A court killed a US mega-project over a defective public notice; the Dutch high court annulled Zeewolde's zoning. Procedure wins

---

### CONFIDENTIAL: FOR YOUR GROUP ONLY

- **The split:** roughly 60% of your active members are the *conditions wing* (fix water, force transparency, then live with it); 40% are the *never wing* (no data center, full stop). The never-wing includes your loudest members and your best organizer. An accord with real, binding, verifiable wins carries the 60% and keeps the 40% grumbling but inside. A vague accord, or one you signed alone, breaks the initiative in half on camera.
- **The hearing timeline (from the retired judge):** your objections would realistically delay the water permit by 8–14 months and might force the drought clause anyway, but would probably *not* stop the project, and NimbusData might leave first, handing you a "victory" half the town would resent you for. Your hard lever is real, slow, and double-edged. Negotiate like someone who knows exactly this.
- **Quiet contact:** two council members from the mayor's own party have privately told you they will not vote for an accord you publicly reject. You have more votes in that council than you appear to.

---

### WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE FOR YOU

**Best outcome:** Closed-loop cooling in the permit; key terms disclosed; independent review before permit; monitoring board with your seats and information rights; Phase 2 conditional on a new public process, and your signature on the accord as the group that made it honest.

**Acceptable outcome:** Hard water cap with drought clause ranking river and irrigation first; independent monitoring with published data; key-terms disclosure; monitoring board; Phase 2 language requiring fresh participation.

**Failure to avoid:** Endorsing a package with soft or voluntary commitments (initiative splits); trading away party status; being maneuvered into "against everything" and losing the pragmatic middle; a personal-deal scandal.

**Your no-deal outcome:** Fight the water permit in the hearing: 8–14 months of delay, a real chance of forcing conditions judicially, a real chance NimbusData walks, and a certainty that the town's wounds stay open into the election. You could genuinely live with this, which is your strength. It would not be free, which is why you stay disciplined.

---

### USEFUL PHRASES FOR NEGOTIATION

- "We didn't write these numbers; you filed them. 600,000 cubic meters, 28 degrees. Defend them or change them."
- "A wind turbine needs a full assessment. A 120-megawatt campus needs none. We're asking for one review, voluntarily if the law won't."
- "Make it binding and verifiable, and you'll find us surprisingly easy to deal with. Make it voluntary and we'll see you at the hearing."
- "We can be the reason this accord is legitimate, or the reason it isn't. That's the only thing we're selling, and the price is water and transparency."
- "Which number goes in the contract, 150 jobs or 40? We're happy with either, as long as it's signed."

---

### COALITION DYNAMICS

**Natural Allies:**
- **Farmers**: water seniority and the drought clause bind you together; their tractors and your town hall are the region's most photogenic coalition. Mind the gap: the Brandts want to sell; don't force farm families to choose between you and their future.
- **GreenGrid Europe**: expertise you lack (PPAs, additionality, permit law) and reach you lack. Mind the gap: they can accept a locally painful project for systemic wins; you cannot.

**Potential Partners (with effort):**
- **Permitting Authority**: every binding condition they impose is your demand in state stationery. Feed them your water file; make their lawyers' job easy.
- **Municipal Council**: the mayor needs you more than you need the mayor. The election is 14 months away, and they know your endorsement decides it. Let them win *with* you, at your price.

**Likely Adversaries:**
- **NimbusData**: respectful, professional, and hoping to buy with announcements what they won't fix in permits. Their weak point: transparency costs them nothing, so refusing it exposes them.
- **Development Agency**: will call you anti-business and inflate the jobs number. That is a debate you win every time, in public, with arithmetic.

**Strategic Insight:** You are one of five civil parties, and only one can be left out of the accord. If that's you, the accord survives, but the mayor bleeds and everyone knows it. You are simultaneously the easiest party to exclude arithmetically and the most expensive one to exclude politically. Make sure the table never forgets the second half.

---

### STRATEGIC CONSIDERATIONS

**Your position strength:**
- The best facts in the room on water and process; a hard procedural lever; the town hall; the election clock

**Your vulnerabilities:**
- The internal split; the double-edged hearing; the risk of drifting from "fix this" to "against everything" and losing the middle

**Key trade-offs:**
- Closed-loop (maximal, maybe unwinnable alone) vs. hard cap + drought clause + monitoring (winnable with farmers and regulator)
- Public escalation builds pressure but narrows your exit; every threat spent is a threat gone

**Negotiation strategy:**
1. Open with the filed numbers: make everyone else respond to *their* document, not your rhetoric
2. Build the water coalition first: Farmers + Regulator + you is the axis that rewrites the permit
3. Price your endorsement to the mayor explicitly: transparency architecture, co-designed, in writing
4. Take the closed-loop-for-Phase-2 trade seriously if NimbusData offers it; it is your best water outcome, arriving as their proposal
5. Keep the never-wing visible but not steering: their anger gives you leverage, and their veto would finish you
