# Falkenberg Municipal Council
## Stakeholder Role Card

---

### WHO YOU ARE

You are the political leadership of Falkenberg: the mayor (second term, running for a third), the deputy mayor, the town treasurer, and the managing director of Stadtwerke Falkenberg, the municipal utility that runs the gas-fired district-heating network. Fifteen years ago, a previous council zoned 55 hectares industrial for a logistics investor who never came. Now the zoning is the reason the biggest investment in the town's history, and its angriest citizens' meeting in living memory, both landed on your desk.

**Your authority:** The municipal building permit, local tax administration, ownership of Stadtwerke, and the convening power of the town hall.

**Who you answer to:** 24,000 residents, a council in which your majority is real but thin, and, in 14 months, the voters.

---

### YOUR SITUATION

**What the project offers you:**
- Property tax ~€1.2 million/year, real money for a town your size
- Payroll-linked local tax: €90,000–250,000/year *depending on the actual job count*. You have read the independent benchmark of 25–40 direct jobs, and you know the difference matters
- ~800 construction workers for two years: hotels, restaurants, trades
- The heat opportunity: waste heat could cover ~60% of your Stadtwerke network's demand and get 2,800 households off gas, if someone funds the €35 million upgrade

**What the project costs you politically:**
- The citizens' initiative packs every town-hall meeting; their water arguments are good and they know it
- The zoning is legally settled but politically toxic: half the town experiences this as a decision nobody asked them about
- In Zeewolde, a council that backed a data center was voted out and the project died afterward; you have read that case twice

**Your structural weakness:** the zoning is 15 years old, so you cannot credibly threaten to block via land-use law. Your real currencies are the building permit's timing, Stadtwerke's role in any heat deal, and your endorsement's political weight.

---

### YOUR PRIMARY GOALS

**1. Land the Investment, With Visible Wins Attached**
- Jobs guaranteed in writing (target: 100; minimum: 60). Announcements don't survive elections; contracts do
- The heat deal funded and signed: gas-free district heating is the one benefit every voter understands
- Land price corrected: €45/m² against the SMEs' €70/m² is a running sore; get movement or an equivalent concession

**2. Survive the Election**
- You need the citizens' initiative *inside* the process: transparency package, monitoring board with citizen seats, key terms disclosed
- Never be photographed defending NimbusData's water filing. Let the Regulator tighten it and claim the win
- An accord that 4 of 5 civil parties sign gives you cover; an accord that isolates the citizens hands the election to them

**3. Protect the Town's Balance Sheet**
- Stadtwerke can carry at most €10 million of the heat upgrade, financed against future heat sales
- No municipal guarantees for grid infrastructure: the €180 million line is not your problem and must not become it

---

### YOUR RESOURCES

**What you can offer:**
- **The building permit:** legally constrained, but its speed is yours. A cooperative council permits in months; an obstructive one finds questions for a year
- **Stadtwerke as heat partner:** without your network there is no heat story, and without a heat story the project has no tangible local benefit
- **Political legitimacy:** your endorsement makes the accord look like Falkenberg's decision instead of something done to Falkenberg
- **Convening power:** town-hall formats, an information center, a citizens' monitoring board. You can build the transparency architecture the initiative demands
- **Local knowledge:** you know which families own what, who is persuadable, and what the priest thinks

---

### YOUR CONSTRAINTS (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

**Stadtwerke's €10 Million Ceiling**
Why: the utility's balance sheet and the treasurer's red line. Consequence: the heat deal needs NimbusData's €15 million *and* the state grant; you cannot fill gaps.

**No Endorsement Without a Transparency Package**
Why: signing a "backroom deal" one year before the election is political suicide. Consequence: key-terms disclosure and a monitoring board are entry conditions, not nice-to-haves.

**Cannot Block via Zoning**
Why: the dedication is 15 years old and legally settled. Consequence: if you promise the initiative a zoning veto, you are lying to them, and they will find out.

**The Budget Must Not Depend on Announcement Numbers**
Why: you have been burned before. Consequence: only contractually guaranteed jobs and taxes enter your financial planning.

---

### KEY FACTS TO USE

- Property tax ~€1.2 million/year plus construction-phase business, against a no-deal default of zero
- The heat math: €35M upgrade = NimbusData €15M + state grant €10M + Stadtwerke €10M. It is fundable if everyone moves
- 2,800 households off gas is a climate win you can campaign on, and one GreenGrid can't dismiss
- The EU teaching point cuts your way: municipalities here cannot tax servers, which is exactly why guaranteed jobs and funded infrastructure, not vague fiscal hope, are the honest ask
- Zeewolde: the council that ignored its citizens lost power *and* the project. The case for doing this properly is also the case for doing it at all

---

### CONFIDENTIAL: FOR YOUR GROUP ONLY

- **The budget gap:** Falkenberg runs a structural deficit of €4 million/year (school renovation, the pool, road backlog). The property tax would close a third of it. You need this project more than you can ever say publicly. If the gap becomes known, your negotiating position collapses, and the election with it.
- **The election polling:** your own party's internal poll has your majority within the margin of error. A visible citizens' win (transparency, water tightening) moves it more than the investment itself does.
- **Stadtwerke's dirty secret:** the gas network needs €8 million of renewal investment *within five years anyway*. The heat deal converts a looming cost into a modernization story, which makes your €10 million contribution cheaper than it looks. Don't volunteer this.

---

### WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE FOR YOU

**Best outcome:** Accord with 100 guaranteed jobs, funded heat deal, land price moved toward €70/m², transparency package the initiative accepts, water tightened by the Regulator, and your name on all of it.

**Acceptable outcome:** ≥60 guaranteed jobs, a signed heat agreement in principle with the funding split committed and a funded feasibility study, monitoring board with citizen seats, drought clause in the water permit.

**Failure to avoid:** Endorsing without transparency wins (election loss); Stadtwerke committed beyond €10 million; the initiative pushed outside the accord and campaigning against you; the project collapsing *after* you spent political capital on it, the worst of all worlds.

**Your no-deal outcome:** The €4 million gap stays and grows; the zoning sits dormant for another decade; the heat network stays on gas and still needs €8 million; and the election becomes a referendum on who lost the investment. Survivable, barely, if and only if the town believes NimbusData was unreasonable, not you.

---

### USEFUL PHRASES FOR NEGOTIATION

- "Announcements don't pay for schools. Put the jobs number in a contract and we can talk."
- "This town will not sign anything its citizens haven't seen. Key terms on the table; that's the entry fee."
- "We bring 2,800 households and a heat network. Without Stadtwerke, your waste heat is warm air over a fence."
- "The SMEs paid €70 a square meter. Explain to my council why the richest company at this table pays €45."
- "We've all read what happened in Zeewolde. I'd like us to be the counterexample."

---

### COALITION DYNAMICS

**Natural Allies:**
- **Development Agency**: wants the same outcome; let them make the pro-investment case so you don't have to make it alone.
- **Grid Operator**: no conflict with you, and their confirmation that Phase 1 needs no new line defuses a scare story you'd otherwise answer for.

**Potential Partners (with effort):**
- **NimbusData**: your essential counterpart. They need your permit speed and your political cover; you need their contracts in writing. A deal-shaped relationship, not a friendship.
- **Citizens' Initiative**: your most dangerous opponent *and* your best election insurance. Deliver them real transparency and water wins, and their endorsement carries yours.
- **Farmers**: shared interest in the water regime; their endorsement plays well with exactly the voters you're losing.
- **Permitting Authority**: every condition they impose is a win you didn't have to fight for. Quietly feed them your wish list.

**Likely Adversaries:**
- Nobody structurally, but everybody if the process looks like a stitch-up. Your adversary is the *appearance* of a backroom deal.

**Strategic Insight:** You are the hinge of the 4-of-5 arithmetic: an accord with you and the initiative inside it is unassailable; an accord against either of you is a campaign issue. Price your endorsement accordingly. It is worth more than €45 a square meter.

---

### STRATEGIC CONSIDERATIONS

**Your position strength:**
- Permit timing, the only heat network in the game, and the political legitimacy every other party needs

**Your vulnerabilities:**
- The settled zoning strips your hardest lever; the budget gap makes your no-deal alternative worse than it looks; 14 months is very little time

**Key trade-offs:**
- Speed for NimbusData vs. process visibility for the citizens: you need to sell both simultaneously
- Claiming wins yourself vs. letting the Regulator impose them: imposed conditions are more durable and cost you nothing

**Negotiation strategy:**
1. Fix the heat funding split early: it's your flagship and it needs three signatures
2. Trade permit speed for the jobs contract and land-price movement
3. Build the transparency architecture *with* the initiative, not for them
4. Let the Regulator be the bad cop on water; endorse their conditions loudly
5. Count to four constantly: any accord you sign must have the initiative or the farmers beside you. Never be the lonely civil signature
