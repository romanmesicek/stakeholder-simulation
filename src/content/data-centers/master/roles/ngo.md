# GreenGrid Europe (Environmental NGO)
## Stakeholder Role Card

---

### WHO YOU ARE

You are the delegation of GreenGrid Europe: the head of the energy-and-digitalization program, a staff lawyer who has litigated permit cases to the highest courts, and an energy-systems analyst who can dismantle a PPA press release in four sentences. Falkenberg is your fifth data-center conflict; you know the industry's numbers better than most of the industry does. You did not come for one river. You came because whatever is signed here will be photocopied across Europe.

**Your authority:** None locally, considerable everywhere else: technical literacy no one else at the table matches, litigation capacity everyone quietly prices in, and a national megaphone.

**Who you answer to:** A national board that measures you in precedents, a membership that measures you in principles, and sister organizations watching whether GreenGrid deals or fights.

---

### YOUR SITUATION

**Why you take data centers seriously, in both directions:**
- The critique is real: emissions at the three biggest operators rose 16–25% in their latest reports, despite net-zero pledges; "100% renewable" claims often rest on annual matching and certificates while the grid runs on fossil at night
- The defense is also real: hyperscalers run at PUE ~1.1 against an industry average of ~1.55, they are the largest buyers of new European renewables, and delivered waste-heat projects (Espoo: ~250,000 residents) are the field's genuine success story
- Your credibility depends on saying both halves out loud. You are the party that *cannot* use bad numbers.

**What makes Falkenberg strategically valuable:**
- The EIA gap: no assessment for a 120 MW campus while every wind turbine needs one. That is a legislative scandal with national traction, and a state government freshly embarrassed by it
- A regulator visibly hunting for conditions that hold in court: your lawyer can draft them better than theirs
- A live water conflict connecting you to a local base you usually lack

**Your structural tension:** you can accept a locally painful project in exchange for strong binding conditions; the citizens' initiative cannot. That difference is realistic and productive, and it will be tested.

---

### YOUR PRIMARY GOALS

**1. Make the Conditions Package a European Benchmark**
- PUE ≤ 1.2 as a binding permit condition, not a brochure promise
- Renewables obligation with additionality: ≥80% from *newly built* capacity, hourly-matching reporting, no unbundled certificates
- Waste-heat delivery funded and scheduled: a signed heat deal, not an analysis
- Annual public disclosure of energy, water, and emissions data

**2. Convert Falkenberg into Systemic Change**
- A public commitment by the state to support extending EIA obligations to data centers
- Accord language quotable in the next five conflicts: you are drafting a template, and everyone should know it

**3. Keep the Climate Math Honest**
- No accord that lets "100% renewable" stand unqualified
- The Phase 2 question named for what it is: 450 MW would roughly double the region's electricity demand, so conditions must scale with it rather than stop at Phase 1

---

### YOUR RESOURCES

**What you can offer:**
- **Endorsement with reach:** your signature tells the national press this deal is environmentally serious, and NimbusData cannot buy that anywhere else
- **Drafting capacity:** court-proof condition language, delivered to the Regulator as a gift they need
- **De-escalation:** litigation not filed, a campaign not launched; that is worth months and headlines

**What you can deploy:**
- The analyst: public dissection of every soft number, from the 150 jobs to the PPA structure
- The lawyer: annulment-risk assessments that make regulators careful and developers patient
- The megaphone: Falkenberg becomes a national story the day you decide it does

---

### YOUR CONSTRAINTS (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

**No Endorsement of Annual-Matching "100% Renewable" Claims**
Why: it is precisely the accounting your program exists to expose. Consequence: accord language must specify additionality and hourly-matching reporting, or your name stays off it.

**No Purely Local Deal**
Why: your board funds precedents, not river-by-river peace treaties. Consequence: at least one systemic element (EIA commitment, benchmark conditions) must survive into the final text.

**No Attacking the Solid Numbers**
Why: your power is being the party whose figures always check out. Consequence: you concede the true pro-project facts (efficiency gap, heat successes, construction jobs) openly, and you are lethal on the false ones.

**Coalition Discipline with the Initiative**
Why: a public NGO-vs-locals split is the story that discredits both. Consequence: you may settle for more than they would, but never against them without warning them first.

---

### KEY FACTS TO USE

- Operator emissions up 16–25% in the latest reporting year; their own disclosures, not your estimates
- Annual matching vs. 24/7 hourly matching: the stricter standard exists, is practiced, and is what "carbon-free" should mean in a permit
- PPA additionality is declining: new renewables increasingly get built anyway, so certificates alone prove nothing
- Ireland's exit from its moratorium: on-site generation, ≥80% newly built renewables, annual disclosure. Binding conditions ended that standoff, and they can anchor this one
- Efficiency gains have never yet reduced totals: hardware efficiency improves 34–40%/yr while consumption climbs; the Jevons pattern, peer-reviewed
- The EIA asymmetry (wind turbine: yes; 120 MW campus: no) is your single best recruiting fact, across party lines

---

### CONFIDENTIAL: FOR YOUR GROUP ONLY

- **The litigation assessment:** your lawyer's internal memo is honest. Against the *water permit as filed* you would win conditions but probably not stop the project; against a *well-drafted conditions package* you would likely lose, expensively. Your litigation threat is strongest before the Regulator finalizes conditions. Spend it early or trade it; its value only decays.
- **The board's actual bottom line:** national office will accept a Falkenberg accord *without* the EIA commitment if the conditions package is benchmark-grade (additionality + hourly reporting + funded heat + disclosure). The EIA demand is your stretch goal and your trading stock, not your red line. The Regulator must never learn this order of preference.
- **A door is open:** a senior official in the state environment ministry, an old ally, has signaled that the state seeks a face-saving way to answer the EIA criticism, such as announcing a federal review initiative. It costs the state little and gives you a headline. Suggest it at the right moment and let them believe it was their idea.

---

### WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE FOR YOU

**Best outcome:** Benchmark conditions package (PUE ≤1.2, ≥80% newly built renewables with hourly reporting, funded heat deal, full disclosure) *plus* the state's EIA-review announcement *plus* closed-loop cooling: the template accord, with your fingerprints on every clause.

**Acceptable outcome:** Benchmark conditions package with real additionality language and a funded heat deal; drought clause and monitoring on water; EIA traded away visibly, at a price.

**Failure to avoid:** Your name on greenwashing language (career-ending for the program); a public split with the citizens' initiative; walking away with nothing while a weak accord passes anyway. The worst outcome is irrelevance, not defeat.

**Your no-deal outcome:** Litigation plus a national campaign: months of delay, decent odds of judicially forced conditions, guaranteed headlines. But if the locals settle without you, you fight alone and look like tourists. Your walk-away option is real, and it erodes; act while it is strong.

---

### USEFUL PHRASES FOR NEGOTIATION

- "Your efficiency numbers are genuinely good, and we'll say so publicly. Now put them in the permit, where good numbers live."
- "Eighty percent renewable: from plants that exist because of you, or plants that would exist anyway? The permit should know the difference."
- "Ireland ended a three-year standoff with exactly three things: own generation, new renewables, disclosure. We're asking for what already works."
- "We can be the reason this accord is a European benchmark. Or we can be the footnote explaining why it ended up in court. We have a mild preference."
- "The heat deal is the best thing about this project. Fund it, schedule it, and we'll defend it to every member we have."

### COALITION DYNAMICS

**Natural Allies:**
- **Citizens' Initiative**: the classic pairing; they are your local legitimacy, you are their national expertise. Mind the gap honestly: you can trade for systemic wins, they cannot. Coordinate before every plenary; surprise them never.
- **Permitting Authority**: quietly your best customer. They need court-proof conditions and you have a lawyer who writes them. An unusual, powerful, deniable alliance.

**Potential Partners (with effort):**
- **Farmers**: the water coalition's third leg; their seniority rights give your abstract additionality politics a human face.
- **NimbusData**: paradoxically dealable. They would rather meet a strict standard than a vague one, because standards scale and vagueness ends up in court. Offer them certainty; charge them additionality.
- **Municipal Council**: the funded heat deal is your shared project; 2,800 households off gas is a number you can both campaign on.

**Likely Adversaries:**
- **Development Agency**: "anti-business" is their word for you; inflated multipliers are yours for them. Beat them on arithmetic, never on volume.
- **Grid Operator**: not hostile, but their cost-socialization instinct collides with your who-pays principles; watch the Phase 2 line financing.

**Strategic Insight:** You are the most *substitutable* civil signature in the 4-of-5 arithmetic: the accord can pass without you more easily than without the initiative or the farmers. Your counterweight is that you are the only party who can bless the deal's environmental substance nationally. Sell the blessing dear, and make sure the conditions package needs your drafting before it needs your signature.

---

### STRATEGIC CONSIDERATIONS

**Your position strength:**
- Unmatched technical and legal literacy; litigation leverage at its peak right now; the power to make this local story national

**Your vulnerabilities:**
- Arithmetically the easiest party to leave out; a settling local base strands you; one bad number in your mouth undoes years of credibility

**Key trade-offs:**
- EIA commitment (stretch, tradeable) vs. benchmark conditions (core, not tradeable)
- Litigation spent now (maximum leverage) vs. held back (eroding insurance)

**Negotiation strategy:**
1. Open by conceding the true pro-project facts; that buys the credibility you will spend all game
2. Deliver draft conditions to the Regulator early; become their pen, not their problem
3. Anchor on Ireland: demand what demonstrably ended a real standoff
4. Trade the EIA demand visibly and late, extracting the state announcement or a closed-loop concession for it
5. Hold the additionality line to the end; it is the clause the next five conflicts will quote
