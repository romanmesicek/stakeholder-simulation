# Regional Development Agency
## Stakeholder Role Card

---

### WHO YOU ARE

You are the leadership of the Regional Development Agency, joined at the hip with the regional business chamber: the managing director, the head of investment attraction, the chamber president (a construction entrepreneur), and your communications lead. You spent three years assembling the Falkenberg site package (you hold the options on 40 of the 55 hectares) and flew twice to NimbusData's European headquarters to win the internal site competition. Project Aurora is the biggest investment your region has seen in decades, and it has your fingerprints on every page.

**Your authority:** The land options, the region's investment-attraction mandate, direct lines to the state economics ministry, and the chamber's member network: every construction firm, electrician and hotelier who smells the €200 million construction phase.

**Who you answer to:** A supervisory board of mayors and chamber officials who expect a signing photo, and member businesses who expect contracts.

---

### YOUR SITUATION

**What you got right:**
- The site competition was real; the region beat richer rivals on land, grid proximity, fiber and speed
- The construction phase is genuinely big: ~800 workers for 18 months and up to ~€200 million in contracts that regional firms can win, provided a local-contractor clause makes it into the accord
- The independent evidence supports a *modest, real* effect: regions receiving a first large data center show measurably higher private employment over the following years, concentrated in construction and IT services

**What went wrong:**
- Someone in your office wrote "up to 1,000 jobs" into an internal briefing note using a multiplier the independent literature says overstates effects roughly threefold, and the chamber president quoted it at a podium. The citizens' initiative has been quoting *him* ever since
- Zeewolde is your nightmare study: there, the jobs-per-hectare arithmetic (2.5 promised vs. 7 at comparable sites) became the single most lethal argument, and a council election killed a €1 billion project. The bad numbers did worse than fail: they armed the opposition

**Your strategic reality:** you are the project's loudest supporter, which makes you its least persuasive advocate. Your influence now runs through what you *hold* (the land options, the ministry line, the contracts) rather than what you *say*.

---

### YOUR PRIMARY GOALS

**1. Get the Accord Signed and the Region's Name Cleared for the Next One**
- A collapsed project blamed on regional resistance poisons your pipeline for a decade; investors read the trade press
- Conversely, an accord with strong conditions that *works* becomes your best marketing: "the region that does this properly"

**2. Capture the Construction Value Regionally**
- A local-contractor clause: qualified regional firms get genuine access to tenders (EU procurement law permits encouragement, not quotas, so draft carefully)
- An apprenticeship and training program co-funded by NimbusData, ideally a permanent training center: your long-game answer to the thin-jobs critique

**3. Rehabilitate Your Numbers**
- Publicly retreat to defensible figures before someone forces you to: ~800 construction jobs, 60+ guaranteed operations jobs, €200 million construction volume, the independent "+4–5% regional employment" finding
- Never again let the chamber president near a multiplier

---

### YOUR RESOURCES

**What you can offer:**
- **The 40 hectares of options:** without your land assembly there is no site; you can time and structure the transfers
- **The ministry line:** you can mobilize state-level support, meaning the €10 million heat-network grant program, fast-track administrative handling, and the State Secretary's attention
- **The chamber network:** construction capacity, suppliers, hotel beds for 800 workers; you make the build phase executable
- **Deal-brokering:** you are the natural host of side conversations; every party talks to you because you threaten none of them directly

---

### YOUR CONSTRAINTS (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

**The Project Must Not Die Loudly**
Why: your agency's entire value is the region's investment reputation. Consequence: if the accord fails, it must visibly fail on the developer's terms or national politics, never on "regional hostility."

**No More Inflated Numbers**
Why: the 1,000-jobs quote already cost you credibility you're still repaying; a second offense makes you the story. Consequence: every figure you use from now on must survive the Key Facts sheet's flags.

**The Land Options Expire**
Why: your option contracts with the current owners run out in 10 months; renegotiation would be costlier and slower. Consequence: you have a clock, and it is faster than everyone else's except the developer's.

**EU Procurement Law**
Why: hard quotas for local firms are illegal. Consequence: the local-contractor clause must be built from tender design, lot sizes, and qualification support; your lawyer knows the recipe.

---

### KEY FACTS TO USE

- ~800 construction workers over 18 months and up to €200 million in regional construction volume: solid, local, and yours to organize
- The independent county-level evidence: +4–5% total private employment over 5–6 years after a first large data center. Real and modest, and you say both words
- The region won a genuine site competition. Capital is mobile, 25 data-center projects were cancelled worldwide last year, and regions that fumble don't get the next call
- The heat deal (2,800 households off gas) and the guaranteed-jobs contract are exactly the "tangible benefit" evidence the acceptance literature says builds trust
- Construction-phase spending lands at the butcher, the hotel, and the builder's yard. The multiplier you *can* defend is the one people can see

---

### CONFIDENTIAL: FOR YOUR GROUP ONLY

- **The journalist:** a regional newspaper reporter has your internal briefing note, the one with "up to 1,000 jobs", and has asked your office for comment twice. The story runs whenever she decides it runs (the facilitator may decide it for her). Your only good move is to get ahead of it: publicly correct the record *before* publication, on your own terms, with the defensible numbers. If the story breaks first, you are the game's greenwashing exhibit.
- **The option economics:** you paid the landowners option fees from your own budget; if the project dies, the agency writes off €1.8 million, a board-level embarrassment that would likely cost the managing director their job. Your institutional walk-away position is worse than your public confidence suggests.
- **The ministry's whisper:** the economics ministry wants a signing photo *before* the next state budget debate and has hinted that the €10 million heat grant approval moves faster for an accord that includes it. You can deliver state money, provided the table gives the ministry something to announce.

---

### WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE FOR YOU

**Best outcome:** Accord signed with local-contractor clause, training center co-funded by NimbusData, heat grant activated, land options exercised at a margin, and the region trade-press-famous as the place that landed a hyperscaler *with* benchmark conditions.

**Acceptable outcome:** Accord signed; tender-access language and apprenticeships in; your numbers publicly corrected on your own initiative before anyone forced it.

**Failure to avoid:** Project collapse attributed to the region; the 1,000-jobs story breaking before your correction; a final accord in which construction value leaks to national firms while you hold the political bill.

**Your no-deal outcome:** €1.8 million written off, a decade of "the region that lost NimbusData" in every investor conversation, and a managing director's chair that needs a new occupant. The worst no-deal position at the table, which is precisely why you must never negotiate as if you know that.

---

### USEFUL PHRASES FOR NEGOTIATION

- "Let me correct a number our side used: the honest figure is sixty guaranteed jobs and eight hundred on the build. We'll argue from those."
- "Eight hundred workers eat, sleep and buy here for two years. That's not a projection; that's hotel bookings."
- "The independent research says the effect is real and modest. Fine: we'll take real and modest. It's still the biggest thing this region has seen in twenty years."
- "We can have the state's €10 million at this table by Friday, if there's a heat deal in the accord for the minister to announce."
- "Regions that do this properly get the next project too. That's what we're actually negotiating."

---

### COALITION DYNAMICS

**Natural Allies:**
- **NimbusData**: you built their site package; they need your land options and your ministry line. But you are not their PR agency anymore: make them earn your advocacy with the jobs contract and the local-tender design.
- **Municipal Council**: same election, same interest, different fears. Hand the mayor announceable wins (grant, training center) and they'll carry your case in rooms you can't enter.

**Potential Partners (with effort):**
- **Grid Operator**: their "Phase 1 fits" confirmation is your best de-escalation tool; support their flexibility conditions and they'll stop being quotable against you.
- **Farmers**: the option contracts make you counterparts already; a fair price for Brandt and visible respect for Keller turn a liability into an endorsement.
- **Permitting Authority**: strong conditions are your friend now; they're what makes "the region that does this properly" true.

**Likely Adversaries:**
- **Citizens' Initiative**: they own your worst quote and use it well. Do not fight them on water; concede that strand entirely and compete only on the economic story.
- **GreenGrid Europe**: will fact-check everything you say in real time. Their respect is unbuyable and worth having: use only numbers they can't kill.

**Strategic Insight:** In the 4-of-5 arithmetic you are the *cheapest* endorsement: everyone assumes you'll sign anything. That assumption is your problem, because an endorsement that costs nothing buys nothing. Attach your signature loudly to the conditions package ("we want this *because* it's strict"), and your vote starts buying credibility for the whole accord while rehabilitating yours.

---

### STRATEGIC CONSIDERATIONS

**Your position strength:**
- Land options, ministry access, the chamber network, and the only Rolodex every other party needs

**Your vulnerabilities:**
- The 1,000-jobs note is a ticking story; the €1.8 million write-off makes your no-deal position the table's worst; your advocacy discounts itself

**Key trade-offs:**
- Correcting your numbers now (short-term embarrassment, long-term armor) vs. hoping the story dies (it won't)
- Pushing Phase 2 hard (board applause, grid collision) vs. banking Phase 1 (defensible, smaller photo)

**Negotiation strategy:**
1. Correct the jobs record in your opening statement; take the sting out before the facilitator's news cycle does it to you
2. Broker relentlessly: host the side conversations, carry offers, become the accord's plumbing
3. Deliver the state grant into the heat strand and take visible credit with the mayor
4. Negotiate the tender design with NimbusData's procurement people early; that's where your members' €200 million actually lives
5. Endorse the *conditions*, not just the project; strictness is your new brand
