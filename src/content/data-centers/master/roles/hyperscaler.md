# NimbusData (Project Developer)
## Stakeholder Role Card

---

### WHO YOU ARE

You are NimbusData's European site development team for Project Aurora: the VP of Infrastructure Delivery, the site development director, the energy procurement lead, and the public affairs manager. You have built campuses on three continents. Falkenberg won your internal site competition on fiber, grid proximity, water, and — decisively — pre-zoned land in a Europe where connection queues run 7–10 years.

**Your authority:** Negotiate permits, land deals, infrastructure contributions and community commitments for Project Aurora — within a mandate set by headquarters.

**Who you answer to:** A global infrastructure board that compares Falkenberg weekly against the shortlisted alternative site, and a legal team terrified of precedents: whatever you concede here will be demanded at the next twenty sites.

---

### YOUR SITUATION

**The project:**
- Phase 1: 120 MW, ~€1.2 billion, 2-year build, ~800 construction workers
- Phase 2 option: expansion to 450 MW total — real, but unannounced; you need a credible pathway to it, not a commitment
- Site: 55 ha; 40 ha under option via the Development Agency, 15 ha still owned by the Brandt and Keller families. Without them: 80 MW maximum, no Phase 2

**Your engineering hand is genuinely strong:**
- Design PUE ≤ 1.15 against an industry average of ~1.55
- PPA commitment on offer: ≥80% of consumption from newly built renewables
- Waste heat available at the fence — 3.2 km from a district-heating network that runs on gas

**Your exposures:**
- Water: your filed permit (600,000 m³/yr, 28 °C discharge into a river with a 26 °C summer guideline) is the protest's best material
- Jobs: your "up to 150" announcement will meet the independent 25–40 benchmark in public — prepare for that moment
- Time: every month of delay costs ~€4 million in financing and re-planning; your board's patience is measured in quarters, not years

---

### YOUR PRIMARY GOALS

**1. Secure the Permit Package Fast**
- Target: bankable permits within 9 months; acceptable up to 15
- A package that takes 24 months is a de-facto rejection — the board activates the alternative site
- The agreement rule works against stalling: you need the Regulator, the Grid Operator, and 4 of the 5 civil parties

**2. Keep Concessions In-Kind and Below the Ceiling**
- Preferred concessions: waste-heat contribution, water infrastructure, jobs guarantees, transparency — things with engineering logic that don't read as "payment for acceptance"
- Total package target: ≤€20 million in-kind; absolute ceiling ~€40 million
- Every concession must be defensible as site-specific, or it becomes a global precedent

**3. Protect the Phase 2 Option**
- You don't need Phase 2 approved — you need it *not excluded* and its criteria defined
- A package that permanently caps the site at 120 MW halves the site's value to the board

---

### YOUR RESOURCES

**What you can offer:**
- **Waste heat plus money:** up to €15 million toward the €35 million network upgrade — your single most constructive card
- **Jobs guarantee:** contractually guarantee a floor of on-site jobs plus an apprenticeship program with the region
- **Water design:** the closed-loop alternative (~80,000 m³/yr) exists in your drawer — it costs +€60 million and only pencils out if Phase 2 stays alive
- **Irrigation reservoir:** €5 million for a reused-water reservoir serving the farmers' association
- **Grid citizenship:** on-site batteries, demand response, and a connection contribution for the Phase 2 line
- **Transparency:** annual water/energy disclosure, a local monitoring board, an information center — cheap for you, valuable to them
- **Land price:** headroom to move from €45/m² toward the €70/m² the SMEs paid — for the right signatures

**Additional access:** best-in-class engineering data, reference visits to your operating campuses, and the credibility of delivered waste-heat projects elsewhere in Europe.

---

### YOUR CONSTRAINTS (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

**No Master-Contract Disclosure**
Why: cloud customer and pricing terms are the company's crown jewels. Consequence: disclosure of *key project terms* (water, energy, jobs, payments) is negotiable; the master contracts are not.

**No Precedent-Setting Cash Fund Above €8 Million**
Why: a "community payment" here becomes the opening bid at every future site; HQ approval caps cash-equivalent spending at €8 million. Consequence: in-kind offers only beyond that line.

**No Permit Package Beyond ~15 Months or ~€40 Million Total**
Why: the alternative site exists, and the board compares weekly. Consequence: beyond these lines, exit beats agreement — and everyone should believe you.

**The 15 Hectares**
Why: without Brandt and Keller, there is no full Phase 1 and no Phase 2. Consequence: you cannot bully the farmers — you need at least one of them at the table, and expropriation is politically unthinkable.

### KEY FACTS TO USE

- Your PUE ≤1.15 design vs. the stagnant industry average of ~1.55 — an independently confirmed gap
- Delivered waste-heat precedents: 92% of a university campus's heat in Dublin; ~250,000 residents served in Espoo
- The closed-loop option cuts water use by roughly a factor of seven — your answer to the river argument, at a price
- ~800 construction jobs for 18 months and ~€200 million in potential regional contracts are real and local
- Grid headroom of 150 MW means Phase 1 needs no new line — the €180 million question belongs to Phase 2
- 25 data-center projects were cancelled worldwide in 2025, four times the year before; developers walk — your exit threat is credible because it is the industry pattern

### CONFIDENTIAL — FOR YOUR GROUP ONLY

- **Your real jobs floor:** HQ has approved contractually guaranteeing **60 on-site jobs** plus apprenticeships. The "up to 150" line includes contractors and services; you know the direct-operations number is 30–40. Guaranteeing 60 costs you little — it prices in contractor roles you'd create anyway. Play this card *before* someone dismantles the 150 figure publicly, not after.
- **The alternative site is real but worse:** 14 months behind Falkenberg on grid access, and its water situation is no better. Walking away costs you ~€40 million sunk and roughly two years. Your BATNA is credible, not comfortable — don't let them discover the second half.
- **Closed-loop economics:** the +€60 million is survivable *only* if the accord defines a Phase 2 pathway. Closed-loop against Phase 2 criteria is a trade you can sign today.

### WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE FOR YOU

**Best outcome:** Permits in ≤9 months; water as filed with a drought clause; concessions ≤€20 million in-kind (heat €15M + reservoir €5M); 60-job guarantee lands before the jobs debate escalates; Phase 2 criteria agreed; land at ≤€55/m².

**Acceptable outcome:** Permits in ≤15 months; closed-loop conceded in exchange for an agreed Phase 2 pathway; total package ≤€40 million; key-terms disclosure and monitoring board conceded; 4-of-5 endorsements secured with only one civil party outside.

**Failure to avoid:** A cash fund above €8 million or master-contract disclosure (global precedent); a package permanently excluding Phase 2; a 24-month process; being maneuvered into bidding against your own announcement on jobs.

**Your no-deal outcome:** Activate the alternative site — ~€40 million sunk, ~2 years lost, a public defeat in the trade press, but the program survives. You will walk if the package stops beating it. Say so once, credibly, and not twice.

### USEFUL PHRASES FOR NEGOTIATION

- "We will put a number in a contract — sixty jobs, guaranteed, plus apprenticeships. Who else at this table signs their promises?"
- "Our design is twice as efficient as the industry average — that gap is independently measured, not our marketing."
- "The heat is real and so is our €15 million. Espoo heats 250,000 people this way. The question is whether Falkenberg wants to be Espoo or Zeewolde."
- "We can build closed-loop — seven times less water. But a €60 million cooling system needs a site with a future. Give us Phase 2 criteria and you can have it."
- "We chose Falkenberg. We can un-choose it — 25 projects were cancelled across the industry last year. We'd rather not be the 26th."

### COALITION DYNAMICS

**Natural Allies:**
- **Development Agency**: wants the investment more than anyone; assembled your land options. Feed them *defensible* numbers before they repeat inflated ones.
- **Grid Operator**: Phase 1 fits their published ceiling; your batteries and demand response make their system stronger. A technical alliance that looks apolitical.

**Potential Partners (with effort):**
- **Municipal Council**: needs jobs, taxes, and a heat deal to survive the election. Your €15 million heat contribution is their political lifeline — price it accordingly.
- **Farmers**: the Brandts want to sell; the price and the water regime decide whether the family split works for or against you.
- **Permitting Authority**: wants conditions that hold in court. Offer conditions you can live with before they draft ones you can't.

**Likely Adversaries:**
- **Citizens' Initiative**: your filed water permit is their best weapon. Transparency and the closed-loop option are the only currencies they accept.
- **GreenGrid Europe**: will attack annual-matching renewables claims with real expertise. Additionality and hourly matching language may buy them — greenwashing language never will.

**Strategic Insight:** You need 4 of 5 civil endorsements. The Development Agency is nearly free; the Municipality is buyable with heat and jobs; the Farmers are buyable with price, water, and respect. That's four — *if* you don't hand Citizens and GreenGrid a unifying scandal. Your worst enemy is your own filed water number.

---

### STRATEGIC CONSIDERATIONS

**Your position strength:**
- The only party bringing €1.2 billion; a credible exit option; the best engineering data in the room
- The constructive cards (heat, jobs guarantee, reservoir, transparency) cost less than everyone believes they're worth

**Your vulnerabilities:**
- The water filing radicalizes opponents and embarrasses potential allies
- The 150-jobs number is a time bomb with an independent benchmark attached
- The 15 hectares mean two farming families can shrink your project by a third

**Key trade-offs:**
- Water-as-filed + drought clause vs. closed-loop + Phase 2 pathway
- Speed vs. concession depth — every month costs €4 million, which makes generous-but-fast rational
- Precedent discipline vs. local peace

**Negotiation strategy:**
1. Open with the heat offer and the jobs guarantee — establish the constructive frame before water dominates
2. Trade water design against Phase 2 explicitly; never concede both for nothing
3. Give the Municipality its public wins (transparency, monitoring board) — they cost you nothing and buy election cover
4. Keep the land-price headroom for the Farmers' endgame
5. State the exit option once, calmly, with the industry record behind it — then never mention it again
