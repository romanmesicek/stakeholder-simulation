# Role Overview & Deal Space

For the facilitator only. The game's calibration numbers, every group's outcome ladder, and where the deal space actually lies.

*ZOPA = Zone of Possible Agreement: the range where the parties' acceptable outcomes overlap, so a deal is possible. Outside it, at least one party is better off walking away (its BATNA / no-deal outcome).*

Every card carries a "Your no-deal outcome" line stating the group's walk-away alternative. The shared no-deal default: NimbusData activates its shortlisted alternative site in another region within months; Falkenberg keeps the dormant zoning, an open budget gap, and a poisoned local debate, while the farmers' land question stays unresolved.

## Who regulates what (the institutional map)

Keep this straight when students blur the gates: **Municipality** = building permit (constrained by the old zoning). **State Permitting Authority** (played) = water + operating permits; its energy conditions (PUE, renewables, waste heat, disclosure) hang on the operating permit under the national Energy Efficiency Act. **Grid Operator** (played) = the connection itself, under the framework of the National Energy Authority (the federal energy regulator, not played), which owns tariff and who-pays decisions and appears in the game only as the Grid Operator's off-stage principal and via event E1. **Politics** = the state government (your State-Secretary side-role: heat grant, fast-track pressure, the EIA-reform announcement) and, locally, the council. Politics can fund, lobby and legislate, but cannot lawfully instruct the Permitting Authority in a pending case; students playing the Authority should push back on attempted instructions, on the record. Telecom regulation is irrelevant to data centers; if it comes up, that is a factual error to correct in the debrief.

## The agreement rule

The Falkenberg Accord stands only if (a) the Regulator accepts the conditions package, (b) the Grid Operator confirms connection feasibility under the agreed cost allocation, and (c) at least 4 of the 5 civil parties (Municipality, Citizens, NGO, Development Agency, Farmers) endorse it. NimbusData must decide whether the resulting package still beats its alternative site. Every group is veto-adjacent; nobody can be ignored twice.

## Where the deal space lies

**Water strand.** The permit as filed (600,000 m³, 28 °C) is unacceptable to Citizens, Farmers and, during live protest, the Regulator. Closed-loop (+€60 M) exceeds the developer's concession appetite *unless* traded against a Phase 2 pathway. The two stable landings: closed-loop ↔ Phase 2 criteria agreed now, or filed withdrawal ↔ hard drought clause + irrigation seniority + independent monitoring. Either can carry a deal; neither is free.

**Heat strand.** The €35 M upgrade is the game's constructive-sum engine: €15 M (developer) + €10 M (state grant, confirmed by event E6) + €10 M (Stadtwerke via heat sales) is fundable and buys Municipality *and* NGO. Its failure mode is the Zeewolde pattern: heat "free at the fence" with nobody funding the pipes.

**Grid strand.** Phase 1 fits under the 150 MW ceiling: the Grid Operator's endorsement is cheap for Phase 1 and expensive for Phase 2. A developer-funded battery + demand-response commitment (Phase 1) and a line-financing formula (Phase 2) is the realistic package.

**Transparency strand.** Key-terms disclosure, a monitoring board with citizen/farmer seats, annual water/energy reporting: near-zero cost to the developer, decisive for Citizens and Municipality. If the developer refuses these, they are protecting a global precedent, not this project; name that in the debrief.

**The linkage deals that carry the game:** heat ↔ municipal endorsement; water regime ↔ citizens + farmers + regulator; Phase 2 pathway ↔ closed-loop or grid financing; jobs guarantee + disclosure ↔ business + municipality cover.

**Where it may legitimately fail:** Phase 2 committed now (citizens demand a new process, developer wants certainty); a cash-fund demand the developer cannot set as precedent; the "no data center ever" faction pushing the initiative past its acceptable line; or E8 expiring before the package closes.
