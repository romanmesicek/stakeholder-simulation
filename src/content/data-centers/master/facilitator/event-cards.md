# Event Cards

Events the facilitator (in the side-role of State Secretary) can inject to shift leverage, break deadlocks, or add realism. Read the card text aloud as "breaking news". Cards are asymmetric by design: they shift leverage, they do not reset the game. Every card is modeled on a documented event from the real European record.

## Overview

| No. | Event | Effect |
|-----|-------|--------|
| E1 | **Conditions menu:** National regulators publish a model conditions framework for data-center permits | Gives the table a reference document; strengthens Regulator and NGO |
| E2 | **Court ruling next door:** A neighboring district's industrial-park permit is annulled over a defective public notice | Procedure becomes real; strengthens Citizens and Farmers, disciplines the Regulator |
| E3 | **Drought warning:** The meteorological service issues a seasonal drought outlook for the Verla basin | Water moves to the top of the agenda; strengthens the water coalition |
| E4 | **The 600 MW leak:** A trade portal reports a NimbusData grid pre-application inquiry far above 450 MW | Trust crisis; hardens Phase 2 language demands |
| E5 | **Poll shock:** The regional paper puts the mayor's council majority within the margin of error | Municipality wobbles; Citizens' electoral leverage becomes visible |
| E6 | **Heat grant confirmed:** The state launches a €10 M district-heating decarbonization grant; applications close in 3 months | Deal enabler: makes the €35 M heat split fundable, adds a deadline |
| E7 | **Independent audit:** An engineering audit of NimbusData's flagship campus confirms its PUE and water claims | Strengthens the developer's credibility on the constructive cards |
| E8 | **The jobs memo:** The regional paper publishes the Development Agency's internal "up to 1,000 jobs" note next to the independent 25–40 benchmark | Forces the honest-numbers reset; humbles the Agency |
| E9 | **Board deadline:** NimbusData's board shortlists the alternative site and sets a six-week decision window | Endgame pressure on everyone; the threat that is also an ending |

## Usage Recommendations

**When to use?**
- When negotiations stall, one strand devours all attention, or a party is being steamrolled
- Best window: the caucus break (Phase 4) and early in Round 2; never during the closing conference

**Sequencing:**
- E1/E3 work early; they organize the water and conditions debate before positions harden
- E2/E5 help when Citizens or Farmers are being ignored; E7 helps when NimbusData is treated as a cartoon villain
- E4/E8 are trust shocks. Use them only if the game is too harmonious, and expect 10 minutes of turbulence
- E6 is the constructive closer when the heat strand needs money; E9 is the pressure closer when nobody will move first. Do not use both
- E8 honors the journalist referenced in the Development Agency's confidential brief; if the Agency has already corrected its numbers publicly, the card is spent, so acknowledge their move instead

**Important:** Use at most 2 cards per session. Pick what fits the state of play; no card is mandatory. After injecting, give groups 2 minutes to caucus before negotiations resume.

## Detailed Descriptions

### E1: Conditions menu (the template arrives)

> Breaking news: The National Energy Authority, together with the European association of energy and environmental regulators, has published a model conditions framework for hyperscale data-center permits: efficiency ceiling PUE ≤ 1.2, majority sourcing from newly built renewables, water caps with drought provisions, waste-heat readiness, and annual public disclosure. Several states have announced they will apply it.

**Effect:** The Regulator's wish list becomes the published mainstream, not a local invention. The NGO's benchmark demands gain institutional backing; NimbusData must argue against a standard, not a stakeholder. Modeled on the Irish CRU decision of 2025.

### E2: Court ruling next door (procedure becomes real)

> Breaking news: The state administrative court has annulled the permit for a logistics park in a neighboring district because the public notice was defective and the affected residents were not properly heard. The project had been under construction for a year. Legal commentators call it "a warning to every fast-tracked procedure in the state."

**Effect:** Party status and clean procedure stop being abstractions. Citizens and Farmers gain quiet power; the Regulator gains an unanswerable argument against political pressure for shortcuts. Modeled on the PW Digital Gateway litigation and the Dutch Council of State's Zeewolde annulment.

### E3: Drought warning (the dry August arrives early)

> Breaking news: The national meteorological service projects a significant drought risk for the Verla basin this summer. The irrigation association has been asked to prepare voluntary curtailment scenarios. River temperatures are expected to approach ecological limits by late July.

**Effect:** The gap in the filed permit (no drought clause) becomes the room's most visible fact. The water coalition (Citizens + Farmers + Regulator) gains momentum; NimbusData's closed-loop option suddenly looks like cheap insurance. Modeled on the Middenmeer drought year.

### E4: The 600 MW leak (how big is this, really?)

> Breaking news: An industry portal reports, citing two sources, that NimbusData filed a preliminary grid inquiry for scenarios of "up to 600 MW" at the Falkenberg site, well above the 450 MW expansion option discussed publicly. NimbusData declined to comment on "routine planning explorations."

**Effect:** Every party re-reads the Phase 2 language it was about to accept. The Grid Operator's financing conditions and the Citizens' "new process for Phase 2" demand harden. NimbusData must choose between transparency and corrosion. Modeled on the Kronstorf 800 MW report.

### E5: Poll shock (Zeewolde in the mirror)

> Breaking news: The regional newspaper's council-election poll puts the mayor's majority within the margin of error. 61% of respondents say the data-center decision "lacks transparency"; 55% would still support the project "with strict conditions." A local list founded by data-center critics polls at 12%.

**Effect:** The Municipality's confidential fear becomes public arithmetic. The Citizens' endorsement visibly converts into election insurance; a stitched-up accord visibly converts into a campaign gift for the critics. Modeled on the Zeewolde council flip.

### E6: Heat grant confirmed (the missing €10 million)

> Breaking news: The state economics ministry has launched its District Heating Decarbonization Program: grants of up to €10 million per project for converting fossil networks to waste-heat and renewable sources. The first application window closes in three months. The minister's press release mentions "promising industrial waste-heat potential in the Verla valley."

**Effect:** The €35 M heat split (developer €15 M + state €10 M + Stadtwerke €10 M) becomes concretely fundable, with a deadline that rewards agreeing now. Strengthens Municipality, Development Agency and NGO simultaneously; the rare card everyone cheers. Modeled on real state heat-network programs.

### E7: Independent audit (the developer's numbers hold)

> Breaking news: A respected engineering institute has published an independent audit of NimbusData's flagship European campus: measured PUE of 1.12, water use per kWh among the lowest in the industry, and waste-heat delivery to the local network confirmed at contracted volumes. The report notes the campus runs "materially better than its industry's average, and better than several of its owner's public critics assume."

**Effect:** NimbusData's constructive cards (efficiency, heat, closed-loop capability) gain independent backing; blanket "big tech lies" rhetoric loses force. The NGO must do what it does best: argue precisely. The audit does not validate the Falkenberg water filing, and precise opponents will say so. Modeled on the delivered Nordic waste-heat record.

### E8: The jobs memo (the multiplier comes home)

> Breaking news: The regional newspaper has published an internal Development Agency briefing note projecting "up to 1,000 jobs" from Project Aurora, printed directly alongside the independent benchmark of 25–40 direct operations jobs and the research finding that industry multipliers overstate effects roughly threefold. The chamber president is quoted from last spring's podium. The headline: "The Thousand-Jobs Fairy Tale?"

**Effect:** The Agency is in crisis and must execute the honest-numbers reset under fire; every other party's inflated claim is now also radioactive. Paradoxically helps close deals: after E8, only contractual numbers (60 guaranteed jobs, €200 M construction volume) carry weight. Modeled on the Zeewolde jobs-per-hectare debacle.

### E9: Board deadline (six weeks)

> Breaking news: NimbusData's global infrastructure board has formally shortlisted its alternative site and instructed the Falkenberg team to deliver "a permittable, bankable package within six weeks, or recommend reallocation." An industry analyst notes that 25 data-center projects were cancelled worldwide last year, four times the year before: "Developers don't bluff about this anymore. They leave."

**Effect:** The clock becomes real for everyone at once. Municipality, Agency and Farmers (Brandt) feel their no-deal outcomes sharpen; Citizens and NGO must decide what they can bank now versus lose entirely. Use only when a deal is genuinely close; as pure pressure on a stuck game it produces collapse, which is a legitimate but harsh lesson. Modeled on Meta's Zeewolde withdrawal.
